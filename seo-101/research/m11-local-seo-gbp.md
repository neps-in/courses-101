## 1. How local results work (relevance, distance, prominence)

- Opening: "When a customer searches for a business or place near their location, they'll find local results on Google Maps and Search. For example, a customer might find local places near them if they search for "Italian restaurant" on their phone. Google tries to show customers the kind of nearby business they'd like to visit." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091 ("Tips to improve your local ranking on Google"; no date shown; checked 2026-11-09) — **Documented**
- "Important: There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google. We do our best to keep the search algorithm details confidential to make the ranking system as fair as possible for everyone." — same — **Documented** (the line to use against "guaranteed #1 in Maps" sellers; ties to Kavya's agency email in M01.L03)
- "Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity. Together, these factors help Google find the best match for customers' searches." — same — **Documented**. ⚠️ The summary sentence now says **popularity**, but the third sub-heading is still **Prominence** (see Conflicts and Freshness).
- Relevance: "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. To help Google better understand your business and match it to relevant searches, provide complete and detailed business info." — same — **Documented**
- Distance: "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who's searching. If a customer doesn't share where they are, Google uses what it knows about their location." — same — **Documented**
- Prominence: "Prominence means how well-known a business is. Prominent places are more likely to show up in search results. This factor's also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." — same — **Documented** (links → M09; reviews → section 4)
- What Google says helps: "Verify your business … so it's more likely to show up in search results." "Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results. If your business info isn't accurate, your Business Profile might not show up for relevant searches in your area." The list covers full address, "Your business hours, including special store hours", "What kind of business you are", and "Other details such as parking or Wi-Fi availability". It also says "Respond to reviews" and "Add photos & videos". — same — **Documented**
- Categories affect ranking: "The categories you select affect your local ranking on Google." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669 — **Documented**
- Where Google gets profile data: "Publicly-available information, such as crawled web content (e.g., information from a business' official website)"; "Licensed data from third parties"; "Users who contribute factual information (such as addresses and phone numbers), and content (such as photos and reviews), including business owners who claim profiles through Google Business Profile"; "Information based on Google's interactions with a local place or business" — https://support.google.com/business/answer/2721884 — **Documented** (**the official basis for NAP consistency**: Google merges your website, third-party data and user edits, so mismatches can override what you typed)
- Same page: "your business might appear in local search results for people who search for "salons near me" or "salons open now" because you've provided information that includes your address and hours." — **Documented**
- Industry survey (Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, published 2025-11-06): "47 of the best and the brightest local search experts are invited to complete a comprehensive 2-hour survey, weighing and scoring 187 factors". Top 5 for local pack/Maps: 1 "Primary GBP Category", 2 "Proximity of Address to the Point of Search (Searcher-Business Distance)", 3 "Keywords in GBP Business Title", 4 "Physical Address in City of Search", 5 "Business is Open at Time of Search (Business Hours)". Caveat on the same page: "None of the experts have special access to the internal workings of Google's local search algorithm." — https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/ — **Observed practice** (opinion survey, not measurement)
- Same report on opening hours: "When your business is open you can be sitting pretty at #1 in the local results, and then 60 minutes before you close you'll start to drop off". It also warns: "do NOT list your hours other than what they actually are for any brick and mortar businesses … it's also against Google's guidelines." — same — **Observed practice**
- **Course teaching point:** Google's three words map onto what a learner controls. **Relevance:** category, services, description and website content. **Distance:** they can't control it, and must not fake it (see fake addresses, section 2). **Prominence:** reviews, links, mentions and real-world reputation.

## 2. Eligibility, name, address and the guidelines that cause suspensions

- Eligibility: "To qualify for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036 ("Business eligibility and ownership guidelines") — **Documented**
- Ineligible list includes: "An ongoing service, class, or meeting at a location that you don't own or have the authority to represent." "Lead generation agents or companies." "Brands, organizations, artists, and other online-only businesses." "Any business whose address is listed as a P.O. box or mailbox in a remote location." — same — **Documented**. **Persona hooks:** Kavya teaching in a rented community hall she doesn't control may not qualify for that hall. Amara, if she works fully online and never meets clients, is an "online-only" business and isn't eligible. Her path is a website, Bing/Apple brand presence and consistent profiles, not a fake address.
- Agencies (authorised representatives): "Never claim a Business Profile without express consent from the business owner." "Never make false, misleading, or unrealistic claims." "the phone number and website for a Business Profile should always be the single, authoritative phone number and website for the business and be verifiable by the business owner." "always transfer Business Profile ownership to the business owner immediately upon request." — same — **Documented** (Amara as consultant; Tomás taking over from an agency)
- Overview: "To make sure your Business Profile won't be suspended we require that you: Avoid prohibited content and behavior … Reflect your business accurately. Comply with the product guidelines in this article." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177 ("Guidelines for representing your business on Google") — **Documented**
- Basic guidelines (the course's non-negotiable #12 in Google's words): "Represent your business as it's consistently represented and recognized in the real world across signage, stationery, and other branding." "Make sure your address and/or service area is accurate and precise." "Choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business." "There should only be one profile per business, as this can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search." — same — **Documented**
- **Name:** "To help customers find your business online, accurately represent your business name. Your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers." "Add additional details like address, service area, business hours, and category in the other sections of your business information." — same — **Documented**
- Worked example: "Business name: Shelly's Coffee / Address: 3247 Poppy Street, San Francisco, CA 94102 / Hours: Open 24 hours / Category: Coffee shop". — same — **Documented** (model for Nila: name "Nila Coffee", not "Nila Coffee – Best Coffee Roasters Bengaluru")
- "Including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile." — same — **Documented**
- Forbidden name parts (headings): "Marketing taglines", "Store codes", "Trademark/registered signs", "Fully capitalized words", "Business hours information", "Phone numbers or website URL's", "Special characters (e.g. %$@/") or irrelevant legal terms (e.g. LLC, LTD, INC)", "Service or product information", "Location information", "Containment information", "Repeated Bilingual Names / Script Transliterations". Examples: not "Midas Auto Service Experts" but "Midas"; not "Equinox near SOHO" but "Equinox SOHO"; not "Burger King バーガーキング" but "Burger King". — same — **Documented**. **India note:** the bilingual rule means "Nila Coffee நிலா காபி" (Tamil) or Kannada/Hindi repeats aren't allowed "even if it appears this way on physical storefront signage".
- Legal terms exception: "To include special characters or legal terms in your Business Profile name, you must provide real-world proof (e.g. signage, business cards, invoices) that consistently displays those elements as part of your business name." — same — **Documented** (so "Pvt Ltd" only if it's on the signage)
- **Address:** "Use a precise, accurate address and/or service area to describe your business location. P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable." "Create your Business Profile for your actual, real-world location." — same — **Documented**
- Indian-address help: "Only include information like cross-streets and nearby landmarks in regions where the official street address doesn't accurately pinpoint the business's location." "If your address doesn't have a street number, or the system can't find it, you can pin your business's location directly on the map." — same — **Documented** (landmarks such as "opp. Adyar Ananda Bhavan" are allowed where the street address isn't precise)
- Virtual offices and co-working: "If your business rents a physical mailing address but doesn't operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile." "Businesses can't list an office at a co-working space unless that office maintains clear signage, receives customers at the location during business hours, and is staffed during business hours by your business staff." — same — **Documented** (**the "fake address" failure point**)
- "Do not include information in address lines that doesn't pertain to your business's physical location, like URLs or keywords." "Do not create more than one page for each location of your business, either in a single account or multiple accounts." "Businesses showing their address on Google should maintain permanent fixed signage of their business name at the address." — same — **Documented**
- Ownership changes / rebrand: a minor name change may count as rebranding. Otherwise "it's considered a new business. You must mark the existing Business Profile as closed and then create a new Business Profile with your new business name." — same — **Documented**
- Promotions: "Any promotion, marketing, contests, or other giveaways should clearly link to the terms of the activity". Also: "Google reserves the right to suspend access to Business Profiles … and may work with law enforcement in the event that the violation is unlawful." — same — **Documented**
- Individual practitioners: doctors, lawyers and others may have their own profile. "Sales associates or lead generation agents for corporations aren't individual practitioners and aren't eligible". For a solo practitioner at a branded location, the format is "[brand/company]: [practitioner name]". — same — **Documented** (optional aside; Kavya as a solo teacher uses her studio's name)
- Keyword names "work" but break the rules: Whitespark's survey ranks "Keywords in GBP Business Title" #3 for impact. — **Observed practice**. Google says such names "could result in the suspension". Teach it as a **rule-breaking shortcut with suspension risk**, not a tactic (see Conflicts).

## 3. Set-up, verification and optimisation (categories, services, photos, posts, hours, Q&A)

### Verification
- "When you verify your business on Google, you gain ownership of your Business Profile and can make sure your business info is accurate." "You need a Google Account to sign up for a Business Profile." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242 — **Documented**
- "Verification methods are automatically determined by Google and can't be changed." "The verification options available to you depend on your business type, public info, region, or business hours. In some cases, you may need to verify your business with more than one method." "If your business is eligible for video verification, we recommend that you use this option." — same — **Documented**
- Methods listed: Phone or text ("Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems won't get the code."), Email, Live video call ("only available within the business hours of your location"), Mail (postcard). — same — **Documented**
- Video call proof: "Show outside signs, nearby businesses, or a street sign that confirms your business location." "Show your products, branded equipment, marketing materials, or tools you use to serve customers." "Show access to employee-only areas or items like a cash register, kitchen, storage room, or non-sensitive business documents." — same — **Documented** (why fake addresses fail: you can't film signage that doesn't exist)
- Postcard: "Most codes arrive within 14 days." "Do not edit your business name, address, or category on your Business Profile. If you do, the code we sent you won't work." "Verification codes expire after 30 days." "Google will never ask for your verification code." — same — **Documented**
- "To help maintain the integrity of your Business Profile, verification reviews take up to 5 business days. On rare occasions, reviews may take longer." Instant verification can happen when "You verify your website with Google Search Console." — same — **Documented** (link to M01.L08 Search Console set-up)
- Re-verification triggers: "If you add or edit an existing category, you might be asked to verify your business again." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669. "If you change your business name after it's verified, you might need to verify your business again." "If you move your business to a new address after it's verified, you must verify your business again." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617 — **Documented** (break-it lab: "fixing" the name and category in one go can trigger re-verification; plan edits)
- India phone check: "In India and some places, we'll double-check your phone number to make sure it's correct. These steps ensure your Business Profile is accurate. To confirm your phone number, submit an appeal." — 3039617 — **Documented** (India-specific)

### Categories
- "Select a primary category that best describes your business. When you choose your category, select a specific category from the list that shows up. For example, instead of "Salon," choose "Nail salon."" "You can't create your own category." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669 — **Documented**
- "Do not select a category for every product or service." Example: "If you have a grocery store with a bakery and deli, select "Grocery store" as your primary category, then add "Bakery" and "Deli" as additional categories." — same — **Documented**
- "You can select up to 9 more categories." "If you select several categories, the category in the first field will be your primary category." — 3039617 — **Documented**
- "Select categories that complete the statement: "This business IS a" rather than "this business HAS a."" "Do not use categories solely as keywords or to describe attributes of your business." "Google can also detect category information from your website and from mentions about your business throughout the web." — 3038177 — **Documented**
- Wrong-category examples from Google: the Super 8 motel with a pool is "Motel", not "Hotel" or "Swimming Pool"; Wendy's is "Fast Food Restaurant / Hamburger Restaurant", not "Dessert Restaurant". — 3038177 — **Documented** (break-it lab pattern: Tomás listed as "Plumber" only when his storefront is a hardware store)
- Category features: "Restaurants: Can add links for online orders, reservations, and menus." "Health and beauty businesses: Can add a booking button to their profiles and update their list of services." — 7249669 — **Documented**. ⚠️ Exact category names in India (for example "Coffee roasters", "Yoga studio", "Hardware store", "Plumber") were **not** verified, because the list is only visible in the dashboard. Learners should type and pick from the dropdown.

### Services, menu and products
- Menu/services: "A menu for a service business like a barber, spa, or car repair shop that lists the complete set of services". "Sample menus that only list "popular items" (or similar excerpts) should not be submitted." "Menu URLs can't be direct links to third-party ordering or delivery services." — 3038177 — **Documented** (relevant to Nila's tasting room: no Swiggy/Zomato link as the menu URL)
- Services editor: "Use the menu and services editor to keep your menu items or services, along with their prices, accurate." "The menu editor is for food and drink businesses only." Both are "(limited availability)". — 3039617 — **Documented**
- Products: "If you run a retail business in an eligible country (US, CA, UK, and IE), you can turn online searchers into in-store shoppers by automatically showcasing your in-store products". — 3038177 — **Documented**. **India is not on that list**, so Tomás/Nila in India can't use in-store products (check again before publishing).
- Reviews can carry services: "When a customer reviews a service business, they may be asked to choose the specific service provided." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050 — **Documented**
- Services and ranking: Whitespark mentions Joy Hawkins research that "the predefined Services that Google suggests on your Google Business Profile have an impact on rankings". — **Observed practice** (second-hand; original study not opened)

### Description, attributes, links, opening date
- Description: "Do not include URLs or HTML code." "Do not exceed 750 characters in the description field." "Focus primarily on details about your business instead of details about promotions, prices, or sales." — 3039617 — **Documented**
- The guidelines also forbid descriptions that "Focus on special promotions, prices, and offer sales" or "Display links. No links of any type are allowed." — 3038177 — **Documented**
- Attributes: "You can edit certain factual attributes, like outdoor seating. Subjective attributes, like whether your business is popular with locals, rely on the opinions of Google users". — 3039617 — **Documented**
- Website field: "Make sure that Googlebot isn't blocked on your site." — 3039617 — **Documented** (ties to M06)
- Social links: "You can add one social media link per platform" (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Pinterest, TikTok, X, YouTube). "Social media links are currently available for select regions and may not be available for all users." — 3039617 — **Documented** (feeds the "same profiles everywhere" part of #12)
- Phone and website must be the real ones: "Do not provide phone numbers or URLs that redirect or "refer" users to landing pages or phone numbers other than those of the actual business, including pages created on social media sites." "The phone number must be under the direct control of the business." "Premium phone numbers are not acceptable regardless of the rate charged to the caller." "Use a local phone number instead of a central call center helpline number whenever possible." — 3038177 — **Documented**
- Chat today: "Your customers can contact you from your Business Profile with text message or WhatsApp. … Chat options are currently available for select regions". — 3039617 — **Documented** (WhatsApp is highly relevant for India; availability not confirmed for India)
- Automated contact: "Google may occasionally reach out using automated messages via call, text, or WhatsApp to the verified phone number on your profile." — 3039617 — **Documented** (warn learners about scams that impersonate Google; the verify page says "Google will never ask for your verification code")

### Photos and videos
- "Your photos and videos show up on Google only after you verify your Business Profile." "When you add an exterior photo, it helps customers recognize your business when they visit." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862 — **Documented**
- Specs: "Format: JPG or PNG." "Size: Between 10 KB and 5 MB." "Recommended resolution: 720 px tall, 720 px wide." "Minimum resolution: 250 px tall, 250 px wide." "Quality: The photo should be in focus and well lit, and have no significant alterations or excessive use of filters. In other words, the image should represent reality." Video: "Up to 30 seconds long", "Up to 75 MB", "720p or higher". — same — **Documented**
- "It can take up to 24–48 hours before your photos and videos show on your Business Profile." "If the cover photo you select is low-quality or if other sources suggest that it's not the best photo to represent your business, a user-submitted photo may be selected instead." — same — **Documented**
- Policy: "copied or stolen photos" violate the Maps policy. — https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7422880 — **Documented** (no stock photos posed as the shop)

### Posts
- "You can post updates on your Business Profile to share announcements, offers, updates, and event details directly with your customers on Search and Maps." — https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169 (old ID 7662907 redirects here) — **Documented**
- Types: "Updates", "Offers" ("requires a title, dates, and time. This automatically includes a "View Offer" button"), "Events". "Tip: Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set." Posts can be scheduled and set to repeat. — same — **Documented**
- "Posts that include a phone number in the post description might get rejected if they cannot be verified as being connected to the business on which they are posted." — same — **Documented**
- Featured posts from social media: "This feature is currently available in English for single-location food and drink businesses in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand." — same — **Documented** (not India yet)
- Posts and ranking: no Google statement says posts improve local ranking. The ranking help page (7091) lists verify, complete info, reviews and photos, but **not posts**. Teach posts as a conversion and freshness tool, not a ranking lever. — **Course inference from Documented absence**

### Hours and special hours

One that only opens for scheduled classes should arguably leave hours blank and link to its timetable. Flag this in the lesson and don't present one answer as Google's.

- Special hours: "You can set special hours if your business temporarily adjusts its hours of operation or remains closed for up to 6 days in a row." "It's a good idea to confirm your hours for official holidays, even if they're the same as your regular hours." "If your business is closed for 7 or more days in a row, or will be closed for an unknown period of time, you should mark your business as Temporarily closed." "For special hours to show up on your Business Profile, set your business hours to "Open with main hours."" — https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076 — **Documented** (India hook: Diwali, Pongal, Ganesh Chaturthi; set special hours in advance)

## 4. Reviews: policies, getting them, replying

### Google's rules (fake engagement, incentives, review gating)
- "Contributions to Google Maps should reflect a genuine experience at a place or business, and a review or rating should reflect an actual experience with a business, and be genuine and unbiased." — https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114 ("Prohibited & restricted content"; same text at https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114) — **Documented**
- Fake engagement includes: "Reviews or ratings that have been paid for, directly or in kind." "Content that has been posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person." — same — **Documented**
- Merchants may not: "Offer incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review or revision or removal of a negative review." "Post content on a competitor's place or business to undermine that business' or product's reputation." — same — **Documented**
- **Review gating, in Google's words:** "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". — same (Rating Manipulation section) — **Documented**. Teach: "Only ask happy customers" (for example, a "Did you love us? → Google; not happy? → private form" funnel) is against the policy.
- On-premises pressure and scripted content: "When soliciting reviews, merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises, nor should they request that specific content be included." This includes "Merchants requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews" and "Merchants requesting that staff solicit reviews that include specific content, including content that identifies a staff member." — same — **Documented** (an "ask them to mention 'best yoga in Adyar'" script is not allowed; nor is a target of "10 reviews per staff member")
- Conflict of interest: "current or former employment, a contractual or consultory relationship, or other professional or personal affiliations … (such as industry competitors, familial relationships, etc.)" — same — **Documented** (family and staff reviews are out)
- Allowed: "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review." — same — **Documented**
- Merchant listing content: "In the listing: Promotional or commercial content that contains any of the following: Time-bound promotions, pricing, or direct calls-to-action." — same (Advertising & solicitation) — **Documented**
- Penalties: "Business Profile will not be able to receive new reviews or ratings for set period of time"; "Business Profile's existing reviews or ratings will be unpublished for set period of time"; "Business Profile will display a warning to let consumers know that fake reviews were removed". — https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287 — **Documented**
- Enforcement in practice: "Our systems are constantly monitoring for unusual patterns in contributed content. When we detect suspicious activity, we act quickly and may implement protections to prevent further abuse. This can include everything from taking down policy-violating content to temporarily disabling new contributions." — Christina Wichiencharoen, Product Manager, Google Maps, blog.google, 2023-11-22, https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/how-google-maps-protects-against-fake-content/ — **Documented** (Google's own blog)

### The law on fake reviews (2022–2025)
- **USA, FTC (final rule announced 2024-08-14):** "The Federal Trade Commission today announced a final rule that will combat fake reviews and testimonials by prohibiting their sale or purchase and allow the agency to seek civil penalties against knowing violators." It covers fake reviews ("reviews and testimonials that misrepresent that they are by someone who does not exist"). It also covers "Buying Positive or Negative Reviews" (incentives "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, either positive or negative"), "Insider Reviews and Consumer Testimonials" and "Review Suppression" ("unfounded or groundless legal threats, physical threats, intimidation"). — https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials — **Documented (fetch-tool extract)**. curl got 404; the fetch tool returned the text. Effective date "October 21, 2024" is from a law-firm summary **(search-tool snippet, unverified)**.
- **UK, CMA "Fake reviews" guidance (CMA208, published 4 April 2025, under the DMCC Act changes "introduced in April 2025"):** The short guide lists banned types: "fake reviews: these are reviews that look or claim to be genuine, but are actually fake"; "concealed incentivised reviews: these are reviews that conceal the fact that they have been incentivised (for example, not making it clear that a review has been written in exchange for money or other benefits, like a free stay at a hotel or another 'freebie')"; "false or misleading consumer review information". It says: "There are other things businesses must not do (such as writing or commissioning others to write fake reviews)." Star ratings count: "a star rating appearing next to a restaurant's name in search engine results". — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews and https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law — **Documented**. The duties to publish policies and run risk assessments apply to **publishers** of reviews (for example, a site showing testimonials), so a café showing reviews on its website is in scope too.
- **India, IS 19000:2022 (PIB, 2022-11-21):** "The Department of Consumer Affairs launched the framework for safeguarding and protecting consumer interest from fake and deceptive reviews in e-commerce". It is titled "Indian Standard (IS) 19000:2022 'Online Consumer Reviews — Principles and Requirements for their Collection, Moderation and Publication." "The standards will be applicable to every online platform which publishes consumer reviews." "The standard will initially be voluntary for compliance by all e-commerce platform." "Once made mandatory, if required, the violation of the standards by any entity may be considered as an unfair trade practice or violation of consumer rights and a consumer may submit such grievances to the National Consumer Helpline, Consumer Commissions or the CCPA." — https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1877733 — **Documented**. ⚠️ It's aimed at **platforms**, not small businesses, and it was voluntary at launch. Whether it has since become mandatory was **not** verified (see Not found). Don't overstate it. A conformity assessment scheme (PIB PRID=1899471, Feb 2023) is known only from a search summary.
- **Course line (all regions):** "Buying, swapping or bribing reviews breaks Google's rules everywhere and the law in the US and UK. In India it is at least an unfair trade practice risk." — **course summary of Documented sources**; keep the hedge for India.

Point out that competitors may report keyword-stuffed names through "suggest an edit" (Maps UGC page: "If you come across misleading information or fraudulent activity on Google Maps related to publicly available factual information, you may edit it").
- **Showing the address helps ranking** (Whitespark "Address Is Showing on GBP (Not SAB)" #7) **vs** Google: service-area businesses "should hide your business address" and virtual offices aren't eligible. The incentive to fake an address is real; the rule forbids it.
- **Hours for classes:** Google says businesses with varied class schedules "shouldn't provide hours", but being "open at time of search" ranks #5 in Whitespark's survey. Kavya's studio sits in the grey zone. Present both and recommend honesty (front-desk hours only if someone's really there).
- **Q&A status:** the Google help text still describes a Q&A section and a US pilot, while secondary sources say it's being removed for an AI "Ask" button. Google's own documentation wins until Google confirms otherwise.
- **Persona details:** course-outline.md says "Tomás, family hardware shop (Lisbon → ships across the EU)" and M01–M04 lessons use Lisbon. M09.L04 quiz uses "best hardware shop in Pune", and this module's brief says Pune with plumbing. Similarly, the outline's Amara is a "junior content writer (Lagos)", while the brief says agency/consultant. **Resolve before drafting** (suggestion: keep Tomás in Lisbon, as the outline is the source of truth, and treat the M09 "Pune" quiz option as an inconsistency to fix; or update the outline if the owner prefers Pune). Kavya is in Chennai in all sources ("Adyar" appears in M01.L05/M10).
- **IS 19000:2022** targets platforms and was voluntary at launch, while popular coverage calls it "rules on fake reviews". Don't tell learners Indian law bans their buying reviews in the same way the FTC rule does. Say Google bans it everywhere, and Indian consumer law may treat it as an unfair trade practice.

## Break-it lab ideas

**Planted profile: "Nila Coffee" GBP draft prepared by an over-eager freelancer. Learners find and fix each error, citing the rule.**

1. Name: **"Nila Coffee – Best Coffee Roasters & Café Bengaluru | Call 98XXXXXXXX"** → keywords, location, phone in the name. "Including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension". Fix: "Nila Coffee".
2. Name repeated in Kannada script after the English → "Repeated Bilingual Names / Script Transliterations" aren't allowed.
3. Primary category **"Café"**, extra categories "Coffee shop", "Wi-Fi spot", "Parking", "Coffee machine supplier", "Bakery" (it buys pastries in) → categories used as keywords/attributes ("IS a", not "HAS a"). Fix: the most specific primary category for a roaster with a tasting room, plus 1–2 true extras.
4. **Three different phones:** the profile has a call-centre number, the website footer has the owner's mobile, and Justdial has an old landline → inconsistent NAP. "The phone number must be under the direct control of the business"; "Use a local phone number instead of a central call center helpline number whenever possible". Fix: pick one primary, update all citations, and log it in the NAP sheet.
5. Address: a **co-working desk in Koramangala** "for a better location", while the tasting room is in Indiranagar → virtual office / co-working rule; verification video would fail. Fix: the real tasting-room address.
6. Description: "Best coffee in Bengaluru!!! 20% OFF this week – order at nilacoffee.example/shop" → promotions, prices and links aren't allowed; 750-character limit. Fix: what Nila is, since when, what's special.
7. Menu URL points to a **Swiggy** listing → "Menu URLs can't be direct links to third-party ordering or delivery services."
8. Hours say **24 hours** to "rank at night" → hours must be "regular customer-facing hours"; Whitespark itself warns against this.
9. Photos: stock images of latte art from a photo site → "copied or stolen photos" are against policy; photos should "represent reality".
10. Review plan: "Give a free 100 g pack for every 5-star review; send the review link only to customers who rated us 9–10 on WhatsApp" → incentives plus selective solicitation (gating). Both are banned by Google, and the incentive is illegal under FTC/CMA rules where they apply.
11. Staff target: "Each barista must get 10 reviews a month mentioning their name" → "Merchants requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews" / "content that identifies a staff member" isn't allowed.
12. A reply to a 1-star review names the customer's phone number and calls them a liar → privacy and personal-attack rules. Fix with the negative-review template.
13. Website: a page "/coffee-roasters-bengaluru-mysuru-mangaluru-hubli-chennai-hyderabad-…" listing 20 cities, plus 20 near-identical city pages → keyword stuffing ("Blocks of text that list cities and regions") and doorway abuse.
14. Schema on the homepage: `aggregateRating` 4.9 copied from Google reviews, and a `telephone` without the country code → self-serving review markup and a phone format that doesn't match the GBP.
15. "Q&A: post 15 questions from friends' accounts and answer them" → "Content that has been posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person" (fake engagement). Also, Q&A may no longer exist.
16. The website link is a `nilacoffee.business.site` page → dead product (retired 2024). Use the real site, location page or homepage.

## Hands-on ideas (free only)

- **Eligibility decision:** each learner places their business (or Nila's tasting room on path B) as storefront, service-area, hybrid or not eligible, quoting the Google line that decides it.
- **Manual local rank check:** 3–5 queries (`coffee roasters near me`, `coffee roasters indiranagar`, `filter coffee beans bengaluru`). Search from 4–6 real spots, or ask 3 friends in different areas to send screenshots. Log the date, place, device, signed in?, pack position, top 3 names, and the "location from" line at the bottom of the results page. Repeat monthly.
- **NAP audit sheet:** columns Source | URL | Name | Address | Phone | Website URL | Hours | Matches GBP? | Fix needed | Owner | Date checked. Sources: GBP, website footer and contact page, schema, Bing Places, Apple Business place card, Facebook, Instagram, Justdial, Sulekha/IndiaMART, Zomato (if food), WhatsApp Business profile, email signature, printed card.
- **Profile completeness audit:** checklist from Google's ranking page (verified, address/area, hours plus special hours for the next festival, category, attributes, photos incl. exterior, description ≤ 750 characters with no links, services/menu, social links, website link to the right page).
- **Review asking kit:** create the Google review link/QR (from the "Tips to get more reviews" page). Write one neutral asking script used for **every** customer (no incentive, no wording request). Add a counter card and a receipt line.
- **Review reply templates (5):** positive with detail; short positive; mixed; negative (apology, explanation, take it offline, sign-off); suspected fake (polite, factual, then report). Check each against Google's tips ("conversational, not promotional", no private info).
- **Category research:** type candidate categories into the GBP editor (or ask the owner), and note the dropdown options; compare the primary categories of the top 3 pack competitors (visible on Maps).
- **Bing and Apple:** import the verified GBP into Bing Places ("Import from Google"). Claim or add the place card in Apple Business (India: "Brand and Location Management" available). Add both to the NAP sheet.
- **Location page draft:** one page for Nila's tasting room (or the learner's location) with consistent NAP, hours, landmark directions, real photos (image prompt), embedded map, FAQs moved from the old Q&A, and LocalBusiness JSON-LD (name, address, telephone with +91, url, openingHoursSpecification, no aggregateRating). Validation is deferred to M12, but learners may paste it into the Rich Results Test.
- **Performance baseline:** screenshot GBP Performance (Searches, Views, Calls, Directions, Website clicks) for the last 3 months. Add a UTM to the GBP website link and find it in GA4 (M08).
- **Capstone:** GBP audit table (issue | rule quoted | fix | done?), 10-row citation plan, review plan (asking script, QR placements, reply templates, weekly cadence), and a manual rank-check log with baseline plus one repeat.
