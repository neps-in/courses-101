# M09.L02 Quiz — "Authority" Scores Are Estimates (DA, DR, Authority Score)

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Moz's Domain Authority page, Ahrefs Help on Domain Rating, Semrush's Authority Score page, Google's "Do you need an SEO?" and Mueller's comments as reported by Search Engine Roundtable.

### Q1. Who makes Domain Authority (DA)? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Google, which shows it in the Search Console Links report
- b) Moz, as its own estimate based mainly on backlink data
- c) Ahrefs, as a measure of a site's organic search traffic
- d) Bing, which uses it to rank sites in its own search results

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** DA is Moz's score, built with a model "primarily focusing on backlink data". Moz says it "is not a Google ranking factor".
**Hint:** Not Google's.

### Q2. What does Google say about third-party SEO tools and their scores? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) They don't have access to Google's internal ranking data
- b) Google approves the most accurate tools each year
- c) Their scores are used as a tie-breaker between sites
- d) Google shares its ranking data with paid tools only

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** "Google doesn't evaluate or endorse third-party SEO tools, and these tools don't have access to Google's internal ranking data."
**Hint:** Outside looking in.

### Q3. Why can a site's Ahrefs DR drop even if it lost no links? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Google lowered the site's rankings and told Ahrefs about it
- b) DR drops automatically once a site is more than a year old
- c) DR is relative; other sites may simply have gained more links
- d) The site's owner forgot to renew its Ahrefs subscription

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Ahrefs' own answer: "Most likely, other websites gained a lot of backlinks." DR compares you with every other site in its database.
**Hint:** Compared with whom?

### Q4. Tomás is offered "guaranteed DA 50+ backlinks, €40 each". What should he do? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Buy a few to test whether his rankings go up
- b) Buy them, but only from sites in the coffee niche
- c) Ask for DR 50+ instead, as Google prefers DR to DA
- d) Decline: it's buying links, and DA isn't used by Google

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Buying links for ranking is link spam (Lesson 9.5), and Mueller: "We don't use domain authority at all in our algorithms."
**Hint:** Two problems in one offer.

### Q5. Kavya is shortlisting 20 food blogs for outreach. How should she use authority scores? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Only contact blogs above DA 40, whatever their audience
- b) Compare them in one tool, then check each blog herself
- c) Average the DA and DR of each blog to get a true score
- d) Ignore relevance and pick the five highest-scoring blogs

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Scores are comparative ("a comparative metric rather than an absolute one", Moz) and differ between tools. Use one tool for a first filter, then check each site is real, relevant and read.
**Hint:** A starting filter, not the decision.

### Q6. Amara's tool flags 60 links to her client as "toxic". There's no manual action. What should she do? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Not disavow because of the tool's label; use Google's Step 0 test
- b) Disavow all 60 at once, because the tool is usually right
- c) Email each site owner and threaten legal action straight away
- d) Buy 60 new high-DA links to cancel out the toxic ones

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Mueller (2023) called disavowing by third-party metrics "a terrible idea". Google's disavow page says "most sites will not need to use this tool" (Lesson 9.5).
**Hint:** Whose label is "toxic"?

### Q7. Spot the error. An agency report says: "Great news: your Domain Rating rose from 22 to 25, so your Google rankings improved." What's wrong? *(O2 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; DR rises only when Google rankings rise
- b) DR should be reported monthly, not as a single number
- c) DR is Ahrefs' estimate; it doesn't show Google rankings
- d) DR is measured in Search Console, not in Ahrefs

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** DR "shows the strength of a website's backlink profile compared to the others in our database". It isn't a Google metric; report clicks and key events (Lesson 8.6).
**Hint:** Whose number?

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m09-l01)* Nila says: "That newspaper link is `nofollow`, so it's completely worthless to us." What's wrong?
- a) Nothing; Google has ignored every nofollow link since 1998
- b) Nofollow links are worth more than normal links since 2019
- c) Nofollow links only count if they come from social media
- d) It still sends visitors, and Google treats nofollow as a hint

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Since 2019–2020, `nofollow` is "treated as hints" (Lesson 9.1). It usually passes no ranking credit, but readers still click, and it's normal in a natural profile.
**Hint:** Readers don't read rel.
