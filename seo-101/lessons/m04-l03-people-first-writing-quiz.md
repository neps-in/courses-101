# M04.L03 Quiz — Writing for People: Natural Keywords, Synonyms and E-E-A-T Basics

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google Search Central (spam policies, SEO Starter Guide, helpful content) and the Search Central blog (December 2022).

### Q1. What does Google say about keyword density? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Nothing: no Google page mentions a keyword density or a percentage
- b) Aim for between 1% and 2% of the words on the page, and no higher
- c) Use the main keyword once every 100 words, starting in the first line
- d) Density matters only for product pages, not for guides or blog posts

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** No Google document sets a density. Google does say that repeating words "over and over (even in variations) is tiring for users", and that stuffing is against its spam policies.
**Hint:** Is there a Google source for any number?

### Q2. Which question does Google say is "perhaps the most important" about your content? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) How many words long it is, compared with the top results
- b) How many times the main keyword appears in the headings
- c) Why it was made, which should be mainly to help people
- d) Who links to it from other sites, and how many links it has

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google says ""Why" is perhaps the most important question to answer about your content", and the answer should be that you're creating it "primarily to help people".
**Hint:** Who, How or Why?

### Q3. What is E-E-A-T, according to Google? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) A score from 0 to 100 that Google gives each page, shown in Search Console
- b) A tag you add to your page's head so Google knows the author is an expert
- c) A ranking factor that quality raters set by hand for every website they visit
- d) Qualities Google's systems look for through a mix of factors, not one factor

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Google says "E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor", but its systems use "a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T". Raters "have no control over how pages rank".
**Hint:** Is it one number, or a description?

### Q4. Amara's client wants a list of 20 town names at the bottom of each service page "for local SEO". What should she say? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) It's fine as long as the list is in a smaller font at the very bottom
- b) It's a good idea, but it should also be repeated in the page's title tag
- c) It's keyword stuffing: Google's examples include lists of cities and regions
- d) It works best if the town names are hidden with white text on white

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google's keyword-stuffing examples include "Blocks of text that list cities and regions that a web page is trying to rank for". Hiding them would add a second violation. Module 11 covers local SEO properly.
**Hint:** Check Google's list of keyword-stuffing examples.

### Q5. Kavya's Search Console shows her page gets searches for "yoga for beginners" and "easy yoga at home". Her page never uses the second phrase. What's the best move? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Add a section on practising at home, using the phrase where it fits
- b) Add "easy yoga at home" ten times so Google is sure of the topic
- c) Create a separate page for every wording of "yoga for beginners"
- d) Ignore it, because Google only matches the exact words on the page

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Real searchers' words show what to cover. Using them where they fit gives "more chances to show up", while repeating them is stuffing, and a page per wording repeats Lesson 3.5's mistake.
**Hint:** Natural, useful, and once is enough.

### Q6. Nila's blog posts are signed "Dr Coffee", with an AI-generated face and no real person behind it. What does Google say? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) It's fine, because Google can't tell whether a photo is real or not
- b) It helps, because a doctor's title adds expertise to every page
- c) It's only a problem on health topics, not on coffee or hobby sites
- d) Fabricating creator profiles like this is a form of deception

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Google's helpful-content page says "Fabricating creator profiles (such as by using AI-generated headshots, made-up names, or false credentials …) is a form of deception." Use a real byline instead.
**Hint:** Read the October 2026 addition about authors.

### Q7. Spot the error: "Our guide is 800 words, but the top result is 2,500. Google prefers long content, so we'll pad ours to 3,000." *(O2 · spot the error)*
- a) Google prefers short content, so the guide should be cut to 300 words
- b) Word count only matters for product pages, not for guides and posts
- c) Google has no preferred length; more useful detail matters, not padding
- d) The guide should match the top result exactly, at 2,500 words long

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google says "there's no magical word count target", and lists writing to a word count as a warning sign: "(No, we don't.)" Add length only when it adds something useful.
**Hint:** Does Google have a preferred word count?

### Q8. Spot the error: "We'll change our live slug from `/cold_brew/` to `/cold-brew-guide/`. Google will find the new URL, so we don't need a redirect." *(spot the error · review: m04-l02)*
- a) Underscores and hyphens are identical, so the change isn't worth making
- b) A changed live URL needs a permanent redirect, or old links reach an error
- c) Slugs can't be changed once a page is live on any platform at all
- d) The redirect should be temporary, so Google keeps the old URL instead

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** As you saw in Lesson 4.2, a new slug is a new URL. Visitors and Google following the old one find an error unless you add a permanent redirect and update your links.
**Hint:** In Lesson 4.2, what happens to people following the old URL?
