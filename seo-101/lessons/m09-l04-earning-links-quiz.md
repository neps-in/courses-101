# M09.L04 Quiz — Earning Links the Right Way

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google Search Central (SEO Starter Guide, link best practices, Do you need an SEO?), the 2017 Search Central post on large-scale article campaigns, the FTC CAN-SPAM guide, and Googler comments reported by Search Engine Roundtable.

### Q1. What does Google's 2017 post say about guest posts? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) All guest posts break Google's rules and should be avoided
- b) Guest posts are fine if each one includes three keyword links
- c) Fine when they inform readers; not when built for links at scale
- d) Guest posts only count if they're published on news websites

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google "does not discourage these types of articles in the cases when they inform users", but objects "when the main intent is to build links in a large-scale way".
**Hint:** Readers first.

### Q2. What did John Mueller say when asked if social signals affect Google rankings? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) "Not directly, no."
- b) That every share counts as a followed backlink
- c) That only Instagram likes are used in ranking
- d) That social signals are the top ranking factor

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Mueller (2015): "Not directly, no." Social helps people discover your content; some of them may then link to it.
**Hint:** Indirect at most.

### Q3. Which kind of outreach does Google say site owners "prefer not to receive"? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) A single personal email about a page they wrote
- b) A thank-you note after they linked to your site
- c) A reply to a question they asked you directly
- d) Aggressive or repeated "Post my article!" requests

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "webmasters generally prefer not to receive aggressive or repeated "Post my article!" requests", and Google encourages reporting them.
**Hint:** Volume and pressure.

### Q4. Nila wants links to her brew-ratio chart. Which plan is best? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Email 400 coffee sites with the same template on one day
- b) Pick 10 sites whose readers use ratios; email each personally
- c) Pay five blogs to add the chart with keyword links to her shop
- d) Post the chart on social media and count shares as links

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** A small list of relevant people, each told once and personally, is the honest way to promote a linkable asset. Paying for links is link spam (Lesson 9.5).
**Hint:** Few and relevant.

### Q5. A food magazine named Tomás's shop in a roundup without linking. What's the best approach? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) One polite email suggesting a link, with an easy no
- b) Weekly emails until they add a link to the roundup
- c) Ask them to add the anchor "best hardware shop in Lisbon"
- d) Report the magazine to Google for not linking to him

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** That's an unlinked mention. One honest request is fine; repeated chasing and keyword-anchor requests are not.
**Hint:** Once, politely.

### Q6. Kavya drafts an outreach email. Which line should she remove? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) "I'm Kavya, and I run a yoga studio in Bengaluru."
- b) "Please use the anchor text 'best yoga classes Bengaluru'."
- c) "Your beginner's guide mentions breathing; our free chart may help."
- d) "No worries at all if it's not a fit for your readers."

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Let them choose the anchor; asking for keyword anchors is a link scheme sign. The other lines say who she is, why it helps, and offer an easy no.
**Hint:** Whose words should the link use?

### Q7. Spot the error. Amara's monthly report says: "Our Instagram post got 900 likes, a big boost to our Google rankings." What's wrong? *(O3 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; likes are counted as backlinks by Google
- b) Likes should be reported in Search Console, not monthly
- c) Social signals don't directly affect Google rankings
- d) Instagram posts are blocked from Google's index entirely

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Mueller: "Not directly, no." The post may help people discover the site; report it as social reach, not an SEO result (Lesson 8.6).
**Hint:** Discovery, not ranking.

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m09-l03)* Nila's backlink audit labels 30 links from scraper sites she never asked for as "Risky — disavow". What's wrong?
- a) Nothing; every scraper link must be disavowed straight away
- b) Scraper links can only be removed by paying the scraper
- c) Scraper links are always "Fine", because more links help
- d) Unasked spam is "Ignore"; Google typically ignores it

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "Risky" means arranged to move rankings. "For known spam sites, Google typically will ignore the links" (Lesson 9.3).
**Hint:** Did she arrange them?
