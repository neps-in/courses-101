# M14.L03 Quiz — How Google Ranks and Updates: Systems, Core Updates and Spam Updates

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-10 against Google Search Central's ranking systems guide, core updates and spam updates pages, the Status Dashboard help page and the Dashboard's ranking history.

### Q1. What happened to the helpful content system? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) It still runs as a separate update twice a year
- b) It was replaced by the reviews system in 2023
- c) It only applies to sites that use AI-generated content
- d) It became part of the core ranking systems in March 2024

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** The ranking systems guide lists it as retired: "In March 2024, it evolved and became part of our core ranking systems". The Dashboard's last helpful content update was September 2023.
**Hint:** Retired, like Panda and Penguin.

### Q2. How long does Google recommend waiting before analysing a core update's effect in Search Console? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) At least a full week after it completes
- b) 24 hours after Google announces it
- c) Exactly 30 days after it first starts
- d) Until the next core update has started

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** "We recommend waiting at least a full week after a core update completes before analyzing your site in Search Console." Then compare with a week before the start.
**Hint:** Complete, then one more week.

### Q3. Where does Google announce core and spam updates with their dates? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) In each site's Manual actions report
- b) The Google Search Status Dashboard
- c) By email to every verified site owner
- d) Only in third-party SEO news articles

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** The Status Dashboard lists ranking updates with start and completion notes, kept for 5 years, with an RSS feed and JSON history. Manual actions are for site-specific penalties.
**Hint:** status.search.google.com

### Q4. After a core update, Nila's brew guide moved from position 3 to 4. What should she do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Rewrite the guide to 2,000 words straight away
- b) Delete the guide and publish a new version
- c) Nothing drastic; leave a well-performing page alone
- d) File a reconsideration request in Search Console

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** For small drops, "There's no need to take drastic action (in fact, we recommend avoiding making changes to content that's already performing well)". There's no manual action, so there's nothing to reconsider.
**Hint:** Small drop, small reaction.

### Q5. Kavya's clicks fell on 3 July 2026. The Dashboard shows no ranking update between 26 June and 18 August. What's the best next step? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Assume it's a secret spam update and remove backlinks
- b) Check her own site, seasonality and tracking first
- c) Wait for the next core update to reverse it
- d) Rewrite every post that lost any clicks at all

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** If the Dashboard doesn't list it, "The issue may be isolated to your web pages". Check tracking, seasonality and technical changes (Lesson 14.6) before blaming Google.
**Hint:** Quiet Dashboard: look closer to home.

### Q6. Tomás removed paid links after a spam update demoted his shop. What should he expect? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) The benefit those links gave is gone for good
- b) Rankings return to their old level within a day
- c) A message in Search Console confirming the fix
- d) A manual review once he files a disavow file

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Google: "Any potential ranking benefits generated by those links cannot be regained." Recovery, if any, comes from the rest of the site complying over months. Algorithmic demotions send no message.
**Hint:** What the links gave, the links took back.

### Q7. Spot the error. A report says: "The May 2026 core update started on 21 May. On 25 May we compared the last 4 days with the previous 4 days and rewrote the 10 pages that lost most." What's wrong? *(O3 · spot the error)*
- a) Nothing; acting fast limits the damage an update can do
- b) Only that the comparison window should have been 7 days
- c) The 10 pages should have been deleted, not rewritten
- d) It measured and changed pages mid-rollout

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** The update finished on 2 June. Google says to confirm it's complete, wait a week, and avoid quick fixes. Measuring mid-rollout mixes old and new rankings, and deletion is a last resort.
**Hint:** When did the update finish?

### Q8. Spot the error. A people-first audit plan says: "Assess only our five best pages, so the self-assessment shows what we do well." What's wrong? *(review: m14-l02 · spot the error)*
- a) Nothing; strong pages make the fairest sample of a site
- b) Five pages is too few, as Google requires at least 50
- c) It should include dropped and risky pages too
- d) Self-assessments must only ever cover the homepage

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** A useful sample mixes your top page, the biggest loser, the newest, an AI-assisted page and a YMYL page. Google suggests looking at the pages "most impacted" by drops.
**Hint:** Strong, weak and risky.
