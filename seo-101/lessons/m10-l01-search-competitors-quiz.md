# M10.L01 Quiz — Who Are Your Real Search Competitors?

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google's site: operator docs, the Search Central updates log, Google's spam policies and Google Trends Help.

### Q1. What is a search competitor? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Any company that sells the same products as you do
- b) Any site that ranks for the searches you want
- c) Any site with a higher Domain Authority than yours
- d) Any business listed near you on Google Maps

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Search competitors rank for your searches, and are often blogs, videos or directories. Business competitors sell what you sell; the two overlap only sometimes.
**Hint:** Who's on the results page?

### Q2. What does Google say about the list of URLs a `site:` search returns? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) It is an exact count of every page the site has published
- b) It shows only the pages that have backlinks from other sites
- c) It lists only the pages that Google ranks on the first page
- d) It is not always exhaustive, especially for bigger sites

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "The list of URLs returned is not always exhaustive. Bigger sites shouldn't expect to see all their URLs in the results." Treat it as a sample.
**Hint:** A sample, not a count.

### Q3. Which Google operators no longer work? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) `related:` and `cache:`
- b) `site:` and quotes
- c) `-` and `intitle:`
- d) `site:` and `cache:`

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** `related:` was removed in 2023 "as it's no longer supported", and "The cache: search operator no longer works in Google Search" (2024).
**Hint:** Both retired in 2023–2024.

### Q4. Amazon ranks #1 for 3 of Tomás's 5 searches. What should he do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Study Amazon's pages closely and copy their layout
- b) Note it as a giant and study sites he could beat
- c) Stop targeting those searches and close his blog
- d) Buy links until his DA is higher than Amazon's

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** A giant scores Chance 1 (Lesson 3.4). Note it, and study realistic search competitors that recur and have pages he could beat.
**Hint:** Realistic, not biggest.

### Q5. Nila's top results for "how to make cold brew" are two food blogs and a video. What are they? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Search-only competitors: they rank but don't sell coffee
- b) Business competitors, because they mention coffee beans
- c) Not competitors at all, because they sell nothing
- d) Giants, because every food blog has a strong site

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** They compete for her searches without selling what she sells, which is exactly a search-only competitor.
**Hint:** Ranks, but doesn't sell.

### Q6. Kavya is in Chennai but checks "yoga classes adyar" while visiting Bengaluru. Why might her list be wrong? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Private windows show results from a random country
- b) Google never shows local results for yoga searches
- c) Local results change with location, so record where
- d) `site:` searches only work from the city you live in

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Results vary by location, language and device. A private window removes history, not location. Record city, device and date.
**Hint:** Where were you?

### Q7. Spot the error. Amara writes: "I'll run a script to scrape Google's top 100 for 500 searches every day." What's wrong? *(O2 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; scraping is the recommended way to check results
- b) Scripts can only check 50 searches a day, not 500 of them
- c) The top 100 is too few; she needs the top 1,000 results
- d) Google's spam policy covers scraping results to check ranks

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Machine-generated traffic "includes scraping results for rank-checking purposes or other types of automated access to Google Search conducted without express permission." Check by hand, or use tools that have their own data.
**Hint:** Check the spam policies.

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m09-l05)* Nila's competitor research ends: "BeanCraft has 400 links from a directory network, so we should buy the same package." What's wrong?
- a) Nothing; matching a competitor's links is always safe
- b) She should buy twice as many links to get ahead of them
- c) Buying links is a link scheme, whoever else does it
- d) Directory links only work for local businesses

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** "Buying or selling links for ranking purposes" is link spam, and SpamBrain may already be neutralising BeanCraft's links (Lesson 9.5). Learn what they earned honestly; don't copy schemes.
**Hint:** Non-negotiable #11.
