> Facts checked 2026-11-06 against Google Search Help and Google Search Central. "Seed term" and "long-tail" are SEO terms, and search techniques are observed practice. Q8's rule was checked for Lesson 3.1.

### Q1. What's a seed term? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) A paid keyword that you bid on in a Google Ads campaign
- b) The exact phrase that a page must repeat in its first line
- c) A short, broad starting point for your keyword research
- d) A phrase that Google has confirmed to have a high volume

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Seeds are short, broad terms from your audience, your offer and customers' own words. You grow them into longer phrases.
**Hint:** Think about what you plant first.

### Q2. What do autocomplete predictions reflect? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Real searches, shaped by things such as your location
- b) The pages that Google ranks highest for each of the words
- c) Phrases that advertisers have paid Google to show people
- d) Suggestions from Google on what site owners should write

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Google says predictions "reflect real searches that have been done on Google". It also considers language, location, trends and your past searches.
**Hint:** Where does Google say that predictions come from?

### Q3. Which statement about long-tail phrases is true? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) They must always be at least five words long
- b) Each gets few searches, but the need is clearer
- c) They're the phrases with the most monthly searches
- d) Google labels them for you inside Search Console

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** "Long-tail" is about how few people search a phrase, not how many words it has. Long phrases are usually long-tail, so length is only a handy clue.
**Hint:** What does "long-tail" really describe?

### Q4. Kavya's site says "hatha sessions", but her students ask about "gentle yoga for beginners". What should her seed list include? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Only "hatha sessions", as it's the correct term for the class
- b) Only Sanskrit names, as these are the most accurate words
- c) Whatever an AI tool suggests for "yoga", in place of both
- d) "gentle yoga", because it's how her students really say it

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Customers' own words are the best source of seeds. People who are new to a topic often use different words from experts.
**Hint:** Whose words matter most?

### Q5. Amara's autocomplete keeps showing a client's brand name, which she searched yesterday. What should she do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Report the predictions to Google as being incorrect
- b) Use a private window, because past searches can count
- c) Add the predictions, as they're the most popular ones
- d) Stop using autocomplete, as it's only for personal use

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Google considers "Your past searches" as well as location and trends. A private window keeps her past searches out of it, in practice.
**Hint:** What makes her results different from a stranger's?

### Q6. Tomás gets 20 phrases from an AI tool. He can find 14 of them, and six appear nowhere. What should he do with the six? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Keep them as Unconfirmed, and don't base decisions on them
- b) Delete them, because they prove nobody searches those words
- c) Mark them Confirmed, because the other fourteen checked out
- d) Build a page for each one, as there won't be any competition

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Rare searches may not show up anywhere, so Unconfirmed doesn't mean wrong. It means there's no evidence yet, so it shouldn't drive a decision.
**Hint:** What does Unconfirmed really mean?

### Q7. Spot the error: "The AI says this phrase gets 12,000 searches a month, so I've added that to the sheet as our data." *(O3 · spot the error)*
- a) Search volumes should be recorded as a yearly figure
- b) Sheets can only hold figures from Search Console
- c) An AI number with no real data source isn't evidence
- d) The number is fine, but only for informational searches

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** AI can produce exact-looking numbers with nothing behind them. Leave it out, and use a named data source, which Lesson 3.3 covers.
**Hint:** Where did the number really come from?

### Q8. Spot the error: "It's got 'best' in it, so it's commercial. There's no need to look at the results page." *(spot the error · review: m03-l01)*
- a) "Best" only appears in searches that are navigational
- b) Every search has to be checked on two different devices
- c) Intent labels must only be given by a keyword research tool
- d) Word clues are only a hint, so read the results page too

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** As you saw in Lesson 3.1, the results page is better evidence than word clues, because it shows what Google currently thinks fits.
**Hint:** Which is the better evidence?
