# M11.L01 Quiz — How Local Results Work (and Is Local SEO for You?)

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google Business Profile Help (local ranking, eligibility, guidelines, service areas) and Google Search Help (location).

### Q1. Which three factors does Google say local results are mainly based on? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Price, opening hours and the age of the business
- b) Website speed, backlinks and Domain Authority
- c) Relevance, distance and prominence (popularity)
- d) Ad spend, photos and the number of categories

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** "Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity." The page's third heading still says Prominence.
**Hint:** Match, near, known.

### Q2. What does Google say about paying for a better local ranking? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) There's no way to request or pay for one
- b) It's possible through a Google Ads budget
- c) Verified profiles can buy a higher position
- d) Only agencies can request a better ranking

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."
**Hint:** Not for sale.

### Q3. What must a business do to qualify for a Business Profile? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Have a website with at least ten pages on it
- b) Make in-person contact with customers in its hours
- c) Pay a yearly fee to Google for its Maps listing
- d) Be registered as a company for at least a year

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** "To qualify for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours."
**Hint:** Face to face.

### Q4. Tomás's Lisbon hardware shop serves customers in the shop and also delivers to builders. Which type is it? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) A service-area business with the address hidden
- b) Not eligible, because it delivers its goods
- c) Two separate profiles: one shop, one delivery
- d) Hybrid: it shows the address and sets areas

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** A hybrid business "serves customers at its business address but also directly visits or delivers to them". One profile per business.
**Hint:** Both at once.

### Q5. Amara writes for clients from home in Lagos and never meets them. What should she do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Rent a virtual office address and create a profile there
- b) Create a profile at home with "SEO Writer Lagos" as the name
- c) Skip the profile: online-only businesses aren't eligible
- d) Create a service-area profile covering the whole country

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** "online-only businesses" aren't eligible, and a virtual office "isn't eligible for a Business Profile" either. Her route is a strong website and consistent profiles.
**Hint:** No in-person contact.

### Q6. Kavya sees her studio #1 in the pack when she searches inside it. Her friend 4 km away doesn't see it. Why? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Google has penalised her profile for one area
- b) Distance from the searcher changes the results
- c) Her friend's phone isn't verified with Google
- d) The pack only shows on desktop computers

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Distance is "how far each business is from the customer who's searching". Check from several places, and log the location line.
**Hint:** Where's the searcher?

### Q7. Spot the error. A rank report says: "We're #1 for 'yoga classes near me' (checked from our office)." What's wrong? *(O3 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; one check from the office is enough
- b) "Near me" searches never show a local pack
- c) The report should use a paid tool's figures
- d) One location can't show where you rank locally

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Results depend on the searcher's location (device, account, IP, even a 6-hour cookie). Log several places, with the location line from the bottom of the results page.
**Hint:** Location, location.

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m10-l05)* Nila's priority list says: "Opportunity #1 — We will rank #1 in the Maps pack across all of Bengaluru by March." What's wrong?
- a) It promises a ranking that distance makes unlikely
- b) Nothing; that's a clear, specific opportunity
- c) Maps rankings can only be promised for one month
- d) Priority lists should never mention Google Maps

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** No ranking can be guaranteed, and distance limits where any business appears. Write it hedged: "We think we can reach the pack within about 2 km of the tasting room, because … (checked <date>)."
**Hint:** Promise vs evidence.
