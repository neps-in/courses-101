### Q1. Which set of four words sums up marketing in this lesson? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Product, design, logo and slogan
- b) Audience, offer, message and channel
- c) Website, social media, email and ads
- d) Research, budget, launch and report

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Marketing is finding the right people (audience), making them something they want (offer), saying it clearly (message) and reaching them where they already are (channel). Option c lists channels only, which are just one of the four parts. Option a is mostly branding.
**Hint:** Who, what, what you say, and where.

### Q2. A person types `why does my coffee taste bitter` into Google. Which stage of the customer journey are they most likely at? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Loyalty: they're an existing customer who wants help
- b) Decision: they're choosing which brand to buy now
- c) Consideration: they're comparing several products
- d) Awareness: they have a problem and want to understand it

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** The search describes a problem, not a product or a brand. That's the awareness stage: the person needs helpful information, not a sales pitch. A decision-stage search usually names a brand or a price, such as `nila coffee subscription price`.
**Hint:** Does the search mention any product, brand or price?

### Q3. What is a **conversion**? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) The main action you want a visitor to take, such as booking or buying
- b) The moment a visitor switches from a phone to a laptop to view the site
- c) Changing your website from one platform to another, such as Wix to WordPress
- d) Rewriting a page in a second language for visitors from another country

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** A conversion is the action that matters most to the business: book a class, start a subscription, send an enquiry, join the email list. Every page should help visitors towards it. You'll track conversions as "key events" in GA4 from Lesson 1.8.
**Hint:** It's what turns a visitor into a customer or a lead.

### Q4. Kavya writes "Audience: everyone who likes yoga" in her snapshot. What's the best improvement? *(O1, O3 · scenario)*
- a) Keep it, because a wider audience means more visitors and more bookings
- b) Change it to "yoga lovers worldwide", so the site can rank in more countries
- c) Narrow it, e.g. "women in Adyar, new to yoga, with back or stress problems"
- d) Leave it blank until she has run ads and knows who clicks on them

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** A specific audience makes every page clearer and easier to rank, because it competes with far fewer pages. "Everyone" is too wide to picture one real person, and "worldwide" makes it even wider. She can widen it later once the narrow version works.
**Hint:** Can you picture one real person who fits "everyone"?

### Q5. Tomás's shop website asks visitors to call, email, follow on Facebook, download a catalogue and buy online, all on the homepage. What's the main marketing problem? *(O2, O3 · scenario)*
- a) There are too few channels, so he should add more social networks
- b) The site needs a lower price, because shoppers compare on price only
- c) The homepage is too short, so search engines can't understand it
- d) There's no single main conversion, so visitors may do none of them

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** When a page asks for five actions at once, people often take none. Choosing one main conversion (for an online shop, usually "buy online") and making the others secondary gives every visitor a clear next step.
**Hint:** How many "main" actions should one page ask for?

### Q6. Amara is choosing a project path. She has no website of her own, and a client said "maybe" when she asked to use their site. Which path should she start with? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Path B, the practice site, then switch to C once she has written permission
- b) Path C, because a verbal "maybe" is enough to start studying the site
- c) Path A, by copying the client's site to her own domain to practise on it
- d) No path yet; she should wait until the client agrees before starting at all

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Path C needs written permission, because in Lesson 1.8 she'll access the owner's Search Console and Analytics data. Starting with the practice site (path B) lets her keep up with the course, and she can switch once permission arrives. Copying someone's site creates duplicate content and copyright problems.
**Hint:** Path C needs permission in writing, not a "maybe".

### Q7. Here is part of a learner's snapshot. Which line has the mistake? *(O1, O2 · spot-the-error)*
- a) "Message: café-quality coffee at home, roasted this week, no barista skills."
- b) "Offer: 12 bags a year, 250 g each, medium roast, sealed foil packaging."
- c) "Main conversion: start a subscription. Second: join the email list."
- d) "Missing stage: awareness. No pages answer beginner coffee questions."

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Line b is a list of features, not an offer. An offer says what the customer *gets* and why it beats the alternatives, for example "fresh-roasted beans every month, with simple brew guides". Features can support the offer, but they aren't the offer. The other lines are specific and follow the lesson's model.
**Hint:** Does the line say what the customer gets, or only what the product is?
