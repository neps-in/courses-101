# M10.L05 Quiz — Turning Findings into Priorities

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against ProductPlan's ICE glossary (observed practice), Google's Gemini Apps Privacy Hub, Google's "Do you need an SEO?" and Lesson 3.4's Value / Chance / Effort score.

### Q1. How does this course combine the Value, Chance and Effort scores? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Multiplies three 1–10 scores into a total
- b) Averages them, then rounds to the nearest 10
- c) Uses only the highest of the three scores
- d) Adds three 1–3 scores, out of 9; ties to Value

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Lesson 3.4's score: Value, Chance and Effort, each 1–3, added for a total out of 9. If two tie, pick the one with higher Value.
**Hint:** Small numbers, added.

### Q2. How does the ICE score combine its three values? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) The three 1–10 values are multiplied together
- b) The three 1–3 values are added for a total of 9
- c) Only Impact counts; the others break any ties
- d) Ease is subtracted from Impact plus Confidence

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Each gets "a ranking from one to ten" and "those three numbers are multiplied". So one low score drags the whole item down.
**Hint:** Multiplication.

### Q3. Which two columns keep each opportunity honest in this lesson's sheet? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Estimated traffic and Domain Authority
- b) Evidence, and Source + date
- c) Word count and keyword density
- d) Competitor DR and backlink total

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Each row records what you saw (Evidence) and where and when (Source + date), because much competitor data is an estimate that ages fast.
**Hint:** What, where, when.

### Q4. Nila's AeroPress guide scores V3 C3 E2; a "coffee" homepage push scores V2 C1 E1. What should she do? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Do the homepage push first, because "coffee" is bigger
- b) Do the AeroPress guide first: 8 out of 9 against 4
- c) Do both at once, since scores can't be compared
- d) Do neither until a paid tool confirms the scores

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** 3 + 3 + 2 = 8 against 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. "Coffee" is a giant's search (Chance 1), a good "won't do" candidate.
**Hint:** Add them up.

### Q5. Amara asks a chatbot how much traffic a rival gets, and it says "30% from Instagram". What should she do? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Use it, because chatbots read every site's analytics
- b) Round it to 25% to be on the safe side, then use it
- c) Not use it; AI can hallucinate numbers as fact
- d) Use it only if the chatbot sounds very confident

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google's Gemini help: AI "can hallucinate and present inaccurate information as factual". Only use numbers from a named tool, with a date.
**Hint:** Your data in, no numbers out.

### Q6. Tomás's top 10 opportunities are all new blog posts. What should he change? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Mix in rewrites, earnable links and a stretch target
- b) Nothing; new blog posts always score the highest
- c) Remove the quick wins and keep only stretch targets
- d) Replace them with ten guest posts on other sites

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** A good list mixes quick wins and stretch targets, and draws on the teardown and link gap as well as content gaps.
**Hint:** A mix.

### Q7. Spot the error. A finding reads: "This guide will rank #1 by March, bringing 5,000 visits." What's wrong? *(O3 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; that's a clear, specific finding
- b) It should say #1 by February to be bolder
- c) Visits should be counted in Search Console
- d) It promises a rank and states a guess as fact

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." Write it hedged: "We think this could reach the top 5, because … (checked <date>)."
**Hint:** Promise vs evidence.

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m10-l04)* Nila's Link gap labels a site that sells "DA 60 guest posts" to two competitors as "Earnable: pay less than they did." What's wrong?
- a) Nothing; if two competitors use it, it's safe
- b) It should be "Not for us", as the DA is too low
- c) It's a scheme: buying links breaks the spam policy
- d) She should pay more than they did to get ahead

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** "Buying or selling links for ranking purposes" is link spam (Lesson 9.5). Label it Scheme, and make it a good "won't do" item.
**Hint:** Never copy schemes.
