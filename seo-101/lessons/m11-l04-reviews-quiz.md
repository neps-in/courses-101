# M11.L04 Quiz — Reviews: Get Them Honestly, Reply Well, Stay Legal

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google's Maps content policy, Google Business Profile Help (reviews tips, replies, reporting, fake-engagement restrictions), the FTC (2024) and the UK CMA (2025).

### Q1. What does Google allow when asking for reviews? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) A small discount for any review, whatever the rating
- b) Asking only the customers who said they were happy
- c) Asking for a genuine review, with no reward or steering
- d) Asking staff to collect a set number of reviews each

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Merchants may "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."
**Hint:** Genuine, unrewarded, unsteered.

### Q2. Which is a possible Google restriction for fake engagement? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) A small fee added to the next Google Ads invoice
- b) A public warning that fake reviews were removed
- c) A permanent ban on the owner using Google Search
- d) The profile's category is changed by Google staff

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Restrictions include blocking new reviews "for set period of time", unpublishing existing ones, and a warning "to let consumers know that fake reviews were removed".
**Hint:** Customers get told.

### Q3. When can Google remove a review you report? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) Whenever the owner says it's unfair to them
- b) After three reports from the same business
- c) If the rating is lower than the profile average
- d) Only when it breaks Google's content policies

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "You can report any review, but only those that violate Google policies are eligible for removal." "Do not report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it."
**Hint:** Rules, not feelings.

### Q4. Kavya's desk offers "₹200 off your next class for a Google review — any rating is fine". Allowed? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) No: a discount for any review is an incentive
- b) Yes, because she doesn't ask for five stars
- c) Yes, if the discount is under ₹500 in value
- d) Only if the reviews mention the discount used

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Merchants may not "Offer incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services – in exchange for posting any review". The rating doesn't matter.
**Hint:** Any reward.

### Q5. A fair 1-star review says Nila's Saturday queue was 25 minutes. What's the best response? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Report it to Google as spam so it gets removed
- b) Reply with the customer's order and the exact time
- c) Thank them, apologise, say what's changing, offer to talk
- d) Ask friends to post 5-star reviews to balance it out

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google's tips: "Apologize when appropriate", "Keep it short and simple", never share private information, and take complex issues offline. It doesn't break a policy, so don't report it.
**Hint:** Fair complaint, kind reply.

### Q6. Tomás wants more reviews. Which plan follows Google's rules? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) A QR code on every receipt with one neutral line
- b) A prize draw entry for each new 5-star review
- c) A tablet at the till that staff hand only to fans
- d) A monthly target of 15 reviews for every staff member

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Google suggests asking customers to "visit a Google link or scan a QR code". Every customer, same ask, no reward, no gating, no quotas.
**Hint:** Same for everyone.

### Q7. Spot the error. A review tool's flow reads: "How was your visit? 4–5 stars → Google review page. 1–3 stars → private feedback form." What's wrong? *(O1 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; it keeps unhappy customers off Google
- b) It's gating: only happy customers get the ask
- c) Feedback forms must only be used on the website
- d) The star cut-off should be 3 stars, not 4

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Merchants may not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". Send everyone to the same review link.
**Hint:** Who gets asked?

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m11-l03)* Nila's profile description reads: "Best coffee in Bengaluru! 20% off this week — order at nilacoffee.example/shop". What's wrong?
- a) Nothing; descriptions are meant for current offers
- b) It's too short; descriptions need 750 characters
- c) Descriptions must be written by Google, not owners
- d) It has a promo and a URL; describe the business instead

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Focus on the business, not "promotions, prices, or sales", and "Do not include URLs or HTML code." The limit is 750 characters, not a target.
**Hint:** Offers and links.
