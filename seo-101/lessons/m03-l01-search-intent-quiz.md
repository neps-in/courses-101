# M03.L01 Quiz — Search Intent: Reading the Results Page Before You Write

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-06 against Google How Search Works, the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (September 11, 2025), Google Search Central and Google Search Help. Results-page signals are observed practice. Q8's mobile rule was checked for Lesson 2.10.

### Q1. What's search intent? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) The number of times that people search a phrase in a month
- b) The words a site owner wants their page to be found for
- c) What someone is trying to get done when they search
- d) The position that a page holds in the results for a phrase

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google's rater guidelines define it as: "What the user is trying to accomplish when they search." The other options describe search volume, target keywords and rankings.
**Hint:** Think about the person, not the page.

### Q2. Which search is most likely commercial? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) "how does a burr grinder work"
- b) "nila coffee opening hours"
- c) "buy burr grinder online now"
- d) "best burr grinder for home"

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "Best" suggests someone comparing before they decide, which is commercial intent. Still, check the results page, because word clues are only a hint.
**Hint:** Who's comparing options?

### Q3. Which three things should you note when you read a results page for intent? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Page types, formats and SERP features in the top results
- b) Word counts, publish dates and link totals in the top results
- c) The number of ads, the page's colours and the site's logo
- d) Domain ages, server locations and meta keywords of the results

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Page types, formats and features show what the results page currently rewards. This is observed practice, and the results can change, so date your check.
**Hint:** What can you see at a glance on the page?

### Q4. Kavya searches "yoga classes in chennai" and sees a map, directories and studio timetables. What should she plan? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) A long article on the history of yoga in the city
- b) A class page with her timetable, location and how to book
- c) A blog post that lists all of the benefits of practising yoga
- d) An FAQ on the meanings of the Sanskrit names of poses

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** The results show people looking for a class nearby. A page with her timetable, location and booking details matches that, and a history article doesn't.
**Hint:** What are the people searching this trying to do?

### Q5. Tomás finds that the results for "tap washer sizes" are about half guides and half product pages. What's the best move? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Pick the intent his site serves best, and focus the page on it
- b) Skip it, because mixed results can never be won by a small shop
- c) Copy the top result's headings, and swap in his own brand name
- d) Build one page that fully serves guides, shopping and local visits

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Many searches have more than one likely intent. Pick the one your site can serve best, and keep one primary intent on the page.
**Hint:** What does the flowchart say to do when results are mixed?

### Q6. Amara's word-clue guess for a search was "commercial", but the top results are all how-to guides. What should she do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Keep her guess, as word clues are more reliable than the results
- b) Ask an AI tool for the intent, and use its answer for the brief
- c) Trust the results, and treat the search as informational for now
- d) Write a page for each of the two intents, to be on the safe side

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** The results page is better evidence than word clues. She should date her note, because what a search shows can change.
**Hint:** Which is the better evidence?

### Q7. Spot the error: "The rater guidelines say a page must meet the user's intent, so raters can push pages up or down in Google." *(O2 · spot the error)*
- a) The guidelines don't mention user intent at any point in them
- b) Raters can only change rankings for pages in English-speaking areas
- c) Only Google's own employees can read what's in the guidelines
- d) Raters have no control over rankings, and they don't move pages

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Google says: "Search raters have no control over how pages rank." The guidelines do cover intent, and they're public, but they aren't ranking rules.
**Hint:** What does Google say raters control?

### Q8. Spot the error: "We hid the booking section on phones to keep the page short. It's fine, because the desktop page still has it." *(spot the error · review: m02-l10)*
- a) Hiding content is only allowed on tablets, not on phones
- b) Phone visitors lose it, and Google indexes the mobile version
- c) Booking sections must go in the `<head>`, not in the `<body>`
- d) Pages with hidden content are removed from Google's index

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** As you saw in Lesson 2.10, phone visitors can't use a hidden section, and Google uses the mobile version for indexing and ranking. An accordion saves space and keeps the content.
**Hint:** Who loses out on a phone?
