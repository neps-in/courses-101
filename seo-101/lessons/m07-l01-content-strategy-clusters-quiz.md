# M07.L01 Quiz — Content Strategy: Goals, Audience and Topic Clusters

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google's ecommerce site structure, links, SEO Starter Guide, helpful-content and AI optimisation pages, GOV.UK's user-needs guidance and HubSpot's 2017 topic-cluster post.

### Q1. What is a pillar page? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) A long-tail post that answers one narrow question in great depth
- b) A page in a special folder that Google uses to rank a whole site
- c) A broad page on one subject that links to more detailed posts
- d) A page that lists every post on the blog in date order, newest first

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** A pillar covers a broad subject and links to cluster posts that answer narrower questions. HubSpot's test: "if you're trying to get the page you're working on to rank for a long-tail keyword, it's not a pillar page."
**Hint:** Think of the aisle sign, not the shelf.

### Q2. According to Google, how does it mainly work out which pages on a site are most important? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) From internal links: more links to a page means more relative importance
- b) From the folder structure of the URLs, such as `/brewing/cold-brew`
- c) From a "topical authority" score given to each cluster of posts
- d) From the number of posts published on the site each month

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** "Google generally doesn't look at the structure of URLs to work out the structure of a site." And: "the more links a page has to it within a site, the higher the relative importance of the page to other pages on your site." Google's docs don't mention a topical authority score.
**Hint:** What connects the pages?

### Q3. What size of topic cluster does this course recommend for a small business? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) 20–30 cluster posts per pillar, as a minimum for any site
- b) One post for every way people might phrase each question
- c) No cluster posts: a single long pillar that covers everything
- d) One pillar and 4–6 cluster posts, each with its own phrase and URL

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** HubSpot's "20-30 posts" is a test of a pillar's breadth, not a target. Google warns that "a high quantity of pages doesn't make a website higher quality", and that a page for every variation can break the scaled content abuse policy. 1 + 4–6 is a course rule of thumb.
**Hint:** Small enough to finish in three months.

### Q4. Kavya has 4 hours a week, and one post takes her about 8 hours from brief to publish. Which 3-month plan is realistic? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Two new posts a week, with fact-checks moved to the end of the quarter
- b) One post every two weeks, plus a refresh and a 4-week check for each post
- c) Five posts in the first week, then nothing until the next quarter starts
- d) One post a day using AI drafts, so that the cluster is complete in a week

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** At 8 hours a post and 4 hours a week, one post every two weeks uses all her time, so any refresh or check work needs a lighter week. The other plans skip checks or need far more hours than she has.
**Hint:** Divide the hours.

### Q5. Amara's client wants separate posts for "best time to drink green tea" and "when should you drink green tea". What should Amara do first? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Search both phrases; if the results are mostly the same, plan one post
- b) Write both posts, with different titles and the same main content
- c) Write both posts, and put a canonical tag on the longer of the two
- d) Write one post and add the second phrase ten times in the text

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Phrases that return mostly the same results are one question, so they belong on one page (Lesson 3.5). Two posts would compete with each other.
**Hint:** Lesson 3.5's overlap check.

### Q6. Nila has a business goal of growing her email list. Which content plan line ties a post to that goal and a reader? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) "Post about whatever is trending in coffee this week, to get traffic"
- b) "Write about latte art, because every coffee blog writes about it"
- c) "Publish 30 posts this quarter so Google sees we're an authority"
- d) "Answer 'why is my coffee bitter' for home brewers, with a sign-up box"

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** It names the reader (home brewers), a real question from the keyword map and the Snapshot's missing stage, and the goal (sign-ups). Google asks: "Are you writing about things simply because they seem trending and not because you'd write about them otherwise for your existing audience?"
**Hint:** Who is it for, and what should they do next?

### Q7. Spot the error. Tomás writes: "I'll move all my garden-tool guides into a `/guides/garden/` folder. That builds a topic cluster, so I don't need links between them." What's wrong? *(O2 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; a shared folder is what turns a set of posts into a cluster
- b) Folders are fine, but he must also add a tag called "cluster" to each
- c) A cluster is made by links between pages, not by a shared folder
- d) Folder names must include the main keyword for the cluster to count

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google analyses "the linkages between pages", and generally doesn't use URL structure to work out site structure. Folders help crawling on sites with thousands of URLs. Moving posts also changes their URLs, which needs redirects (Lesson 6.5).
**Hint:** What does a reader click?

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m03-l05)* Nila's keyword map shows **TRUE** in **Used twice?** for `/cold-brew`. She says: "That's fine, it just means the page is popular." What's actually wrong?
- a) The formula is broken; **Used twice?** should always show TRUE
- b) The URL is given to two topics, so they compete or belong together
- c) The page has two internal links, which is too many for one post
- d) The page needs a second URL so that each topic can share the content

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** **Used twice?** shows TRUE when one URL is mapped to more than one topic. Either the topics belong together as one row, or one of them needs its own page (Lesson 3.5).
**Hint:** It counts URLs, not visits.
