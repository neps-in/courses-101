# M14.L02 Quiz — People-First Content: A Self-Assessment

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-10 against Google Search Central's *Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content* (2026-10-05), the core updates page and the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (September 11, 2025).

### Q1. In Google's "Avoid creating search engine-first content" group, what does answering "yes" mean? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) The page is people-first and ready to publish as it is
- b) Google will remove the page from its index at the next crawl
- c) The page now qualifies to be shown in AI Overviews
- d) It's a warning sign to rethink how you make content

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Google says answering yes to some or all of these questions "is a warning sign that you should reevaluate how you're creating content". In the other three groups, yes is the good answer.
**Hint:** This is the one group where you hope to say no.

### Q2. Which four attributes of main content are raters trained to judge? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Length, keyword use, freshness and links
- b) Effort, originality, talent or skill, accuracy
- c) Design, speed, mobile layout and HTTPS security
- d) Bylines, dates, word count and the share buttons

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** The "Main content" section (October 2026) lists effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy. Length, dates and keyword counts aren't on the list.
**Hint:** Work, newness, craft, correctness.

### Q3. Who does Google suggest should give you an honest assessment of your content? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) People you trust who are unaffiliated with your site
- b) Your own writers, since they know the content best
- c) An SEO tool that scores content quality out of 100
- d) A Google quality rater, booked through Search Console

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Google: "consider having others you trust but who are unaffiliated with your site provide an honest assessment." Your own team has blind spots, tools can't see Google's data, and you can't book a rater.
**Hint:** Trusted, but with nothing to gain.

### Q4. Nila re-tested her cold brew ratios and rewrote half the guide. Should she change the published date? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) No: dates should never change after publishing
- b) No: changing any date is a spam policy violation
- c) Yes, and she should say what changed
- d) Yes, and every other post should get the same date

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google's warning is about changing dates "when the content has not substantially changed". Here it has, so updating the date is honest. Re-dating every other post would be the warning sign.
**Hint:** Did the content substantially change?

### Q5. Tomás has no electrician on staff but wants a wiring guide. Which option keeps the page trustworthy? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Invent "R. Mehta, master electrician" with an AI photo
- b) Publish it with no byline so nobody can question it
- c) Credit an electrician's website at the bottom of the post
- d) Pay a real electrician to review it, and name them

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Fabricated profiles are "a form of deception". No byline on a clear YMYL safety topic hides who's responsible, and a credit at the bottom doesn't replace expert review or original effort.
**Hint:** Real person, real check, named on the page.

### Q6. Kavya picks five pages for her self-assessment. Which set follows the lesson's sampling advice? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Her five best-performing pages, so the results look good
- b) Top page, biggest loser, newest, AI-assisted, YMYL page
- c) Five random posts chosen from her oldest year only
- d) Just the homepage, since it represents the whole site

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** A mix of strong, dropped, new, AI-assisted and YMYL pages shows the site's pattern. Google also suggests looking at the pages "most impacted" by drops.
**Hint:** Cover the strong, the weak and the risky.

### Q7. Spot the error. An agency's quality plan says: "1) Pad every post to 2,000 words. 2) Credit all sources so paraphrased posts count as original. 3) Add bylines linking to real author pages." Which steps are wrong? *(O3 · spot the error)*
- a) Only step 3, because bylines don't matter to Google
- b) Steps 1 and 3; credit is all a page needs
- c) Steps 1 and 2; step 3 is good practice
- d) None of them; all three match Google's page

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google has no preferred word count ("No, we don't."), and "Attribution or giving credit to other sources doesn't replace the need for original effort." Bylines that lead to more about the author are exactly what Google suggests.
**Hint:** Which step is in Google's own Who questions?

### Q8. Spot the error. A writer says: "We used AI for the outline and grammar, so we must put a big 'AI-GENERATED' label on every page, or Google will penalise us." What's wrong? *(review: m07-l04 · spot the error)*
- a) Disclosure is recommended where readers would wonder, not required
- b) Nothing; Google requires an AI label on all AI-assisted pages
- c) Google bans AI-assisted content, so labels can't fix it
- d) Labels are needed only on pages with AI images

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Google says AI disclosures "are useful for content where someone might think "How was this created?"" and to add them "when it would be reasonably expected". AI help isn't banned; scaled, low-value content is.
**Hint:** Useful, reasonably expected; not mandatory.
