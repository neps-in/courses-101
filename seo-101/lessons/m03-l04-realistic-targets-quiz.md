# M03.L04 Quiz — Realistic Targets: Choosing Keywords a New Site Can Win

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-06 against Google Search Central and Ahrefs' own pages. The reading method and scoring are observed practice and a course rule of thumb. Q8's rule was checked for Lesson 3.3.

### Q1. Which sign suggests that a phrase is open to a new site? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Forums and thin pages appear in the top results
- b) Global brands fill every one of the top ten results
- c) Every top page was built exactly for this one phrase
- d) The phrase has the highest volume in your whole list

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Forums and weak pages suggest that nobody has fully answered the search yet. This is observed practice, so read the pages yourself.
**Hint:** What would show you that the current answers aren't very strong?

### Q2. What's the first question to ask about any phrase on your list? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) How high is its monthly volume across all countries?
- b) What's its difficulty score in at least two SEO tools?
- c) Can your site really serve the intent of this search?
- d) How many words do the pages in the top results have?

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Intent is the gate. If the results want something you don't offer, no score can rescue the phrase, so drop it and note why.
**Hint:** What comes first in the flowchart?

### Q3. What does Google say about guaranteed rankings? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Agencies can guarantee them if they're Google partners
- b) New sites get a short ranking boost for the first month
- c) Rankings can be guaranteed for very low-volume phrases
- d) No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Google says exactly that: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." Be wary of anyone who promises positions or dates.
**Hint:** Who controls the rankings?

### Q4. Tomás compares "what is a plumber" (10K–100K searches) with "ceramic tap cartridge 35mm lisbon" (10–100 searches). He stocks the cartridge. Which should come first? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) The first, because volume is the main thing to compare
- b) The second, as it's close to a sale he can really serve
- c) Neither of them, as both are outside his business goals
- d) The first, as its broad reach will lead people to his shop

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** A small number on a phrase worth a real sale can beat a big number that never pays. Volume is a sense check, not the score.
**Hint:** Which of these people is ready to buy from him?

### Q5. All five of Kavya's picks are big, valuable, locked phrases. What's the best change? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Keep all five, since high-value phrases are always best
- b) Delete them all, and only keep phrases with no competition
- c) Keep one or two as stretch, and add some quick wins
- d) Raise their Chance scores, so that the list looks stronger

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** A mix gives early progress and something bigger to work towards. Stretch targets alone could mean a long wait with nothing to show.
**Hint:** How many of each does the lesson suggest?

### Q6. A tool gives a phrase a difficulty of 8, but the top five pages are detailed, well-fitted guides from major brands. How should Amara rate its Chance? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Low, as what she can see on the results page matters most
- b) High, as the tool has already done the work of judging it
- c) High, but only after running it through a second SEO tool
- d) She can't rate it until she has seen a full year of its data

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Difficulty is a tool's estimate. Ahrefs says: "Keyword Difficulty doesn't take into account any on-page factors." Reading the results shows what the score can't.
**Hint:** Which is the better evidence: a score or the pages themselves?

### Q7. Spot the error: "Our score for this phrase is 9 out of 9, so we'll rank on page one within a month." *(O3 · spot the error)*
- a) The total should be out of 10, not 9, for it to be valid
- b) Scores only apply to phrases that have a very high volume
- c) A score of 9 means the phrase should really be dropped
- d) A score records judgement, and it can't predict dates

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** The score helps you choose and explain your reasons. Google says that some changes "could take several months", and that rankings can't be guaranteed.
**Hint:** What is the score really a record of?

### Q8. Spot the error: "Keyword Planner shows Low competition, so it'll be easy to rank in the organic results." *(spot the error · review: m03-l03)*
- a) Competition only applies to phrases of over 1,000 searches
- b) Competition is about advertisers, not organic rankings
- c) Low competition means the phrase has been removed from ads
- d) Keyword Planner shows this only for long-tail phrases

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** As you saw in Lesson 3.3, Competition is "The number of advertisers that showed on each keyword relative to all keywords across Google."
**Hint:** Who is Keyword Planner built for?
