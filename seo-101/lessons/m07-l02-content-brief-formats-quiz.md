# M07.L02 Quiz — The Content Brief and Post Formats by Intent

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google's SEO Starter Guide, helpful-content page, AI optimisation guide, review guidance and ranking systems guide, the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (September 2025) and GOV.UK's user-needs guidance.

### Q1. What does Google say about an ideal word count? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) Posts should be at least 1,500 words to rank for competitive phrases
- b) There's no magical word count target, minimum or maximum
- c) Posts should match the average length of the top ten results exactly
- d) Short posts under 300 words are removed from the index over time

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** "The length of the content alone doesn't matter for ranking purposes (there's no magical word count target, minimum or maximum, though you probably want to have at least one word)." Set scope from your outline instead.
**Hint:** "(No, we don't.)"

### Q2. Which brief field answers "why would someone read ours instead of the others"? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) The scope, because a longer post covers more than the others
- b) The main phrase, because it names what searchers type in
- c) The call to action, because it shows what readers do next
- d) The angle: what only we can add, such as first-hand tests

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** The angle names the unique value, usually first-hand experience, tests, photos or data. Google: "Don't just rehash what others already published."
**Hint:** It starts "Only we can…".

### Q3. What does Google call content "based on common knowledge, which could originate from anyone"? *(O3 · recall)*
- a) Commodity content, which typically adds little unique insight
- b) Duplicate content, which is filtered out by canonical tags
- c) Thin affiliate content, which breaks the spam policies
- d) Evergreen content, which ranks well for many years

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Google's AI optimisation guide: "Commodity content (for example, something like "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers") is often based on common knowledge, which could originate from anyone, and typically adds little unique insight for readers."
**Hint:** Like a product anyone can sell.

### Q4. Nila searches "best coffee grinder for french press". The top results are review lists with photos of tested grinders. She has used only one grinder. What should she do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Write a "best 10 grinders" list from the specs on other review sites
- b) Write a "best 10 grinders" list and add a stock photo of each one
- c) Write about the grinder she uses, or choose a format she can test
- d) Skip the brief and ask an AI tool to write the list of ten picks

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Google's review guidance asks you to "Provide evidence such as visuals, audio, or other links of your own experience with what you are reviewing". Without tests, a "best X" list is commodity content.
**Hint:** What can she show first-hand?

### Q5. Tomás searches "buy cordless drill 18v" and sees only product and category pages from shops. What should he create? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) A 2,000-word blog post about the history of cordless drills
- b) A "what is a cordless drill?" explainer with an FAQ section
- c) A "drills vs screwdrivers" comparison post on his blog
- d) A better product or category page, not a blog post

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** The results show transactional intent, so searchers want to buy. A blog post won't match. Improve the product or category page (Lesson 4.6).
**Hint:** What page type is ranking?

### Q6. Nila's brief tool suggests 2,350 words for "French press ratio". Her outline needs about 700. What should she write in **Scope**? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) 2,350 words, with extra sections added to reach the target
- b) A range from her outline, such as 600–800 words
- c) 5,000 words, so that it's longer than every other result
- d) No scope at all, since length never matters for anything

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Scope is a planning estimate from the outline. The tool's number is an average of other pages, and padding creates filler, which "can artificially inflate content". Scope still helps plan time, so don't drop it.
**Hint:** Count your sections.

### Q7. Spot the error. Amara's brief says: "Angle: a high-quality, comprehensive, SEO-optimised guide." What's wrong with it? *(O1 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Any site could claim it; it names nothing only the client has
- b) It should also say the post will be at least 3,000 words long
- c) Nothing; "comprehensive" is the angle that Google rewards most
- d) Angles belong in the meta description, not in the brief itself

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** An angle must name something only this site can add, such as first-hand tests, photos, local knowledge or customer data. Google asks: "Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?"
**Hint:** Could a competitor write the same line?

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m04-l02)* Nila's cold brew draft opens with three paragraphs about the history of coffee, and the ratio appears in the fifth paragraph. What's the problem?
- a) The ratio should be in the meta keywords tag, not in the text
- b) History sections must always be the first H2 of a how-to post
- c) The answer is buried; readers of a how-to want the ratio first
- d) Nothing; a long introduction is needed to reach the word count

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** An answer-first opening (Lesson 4.2) gives readers what they came for, then the detail. For a how-to, the ratio or first step goes at the top.
**Hint:** What did the searcher ask for?
