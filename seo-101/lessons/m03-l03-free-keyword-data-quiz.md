### Q1. What does the Competition column in Keyword Planner measure? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) How many sites have a page ranking for the keyword in Google
- b) How many advertisers showed on it, relative to others
- c) How hard it would be for your page to rank in organic results
- d) How many links the top ten results for a keyword have earned

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Google defines it as "The number of advertisers that showed on each keyword relative to all keywords across Google." It's about ads, not organic rankings.
**Hint:** Who is Keyword Planner built for?

### Q2. What does a score of 100 mean on a Google Trends chart? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) The phrase was searched 100 times in that week or month
- b) The phrase is in the top 100 searches in that country
- c) 100% of the people in that place searched for the phrase
- d) It's the peak on a relative scale, not a count

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Trends compares each point with total searches for that place and time, then scales the results from 0 to 100. It's relative.
**Hint:** Is Trends counting searches?

### Q3. Which source shows the searches where your own site appeared? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Search Console, in the Performance report's queries
- b) Google Trends, in its interest by subregion map
- c) Keyword Planner, in its Top of page bid columns
- d) The Ahrefs Keyword Difficulty Checker, in its score

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Search Console's Performance report shows impressions and clicks for your own site. It doesn't show the total demand for a phrase.
**Hint:** Which tool did you verify your site in, back in Lesson 1.8?

### Q4. Two tools give the same phrase a difficulty of 20 and 55. What's the most likely reason? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) One of the two tools must be broken, so stop using it
- b) Google changed its official score between the two checks
- c) Each tool calculates difficulty in its own way
- d) The phrase has moved up by 35 places in the results

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Ahrefs says: "These tools all use the same 0-100 scale, but each one calculates it differently." Compare phrases inside one tool, and never across two.
**Hint:** Does Google publish a difficulty score?

### Q5. Kavya's new site has no Search Console data yet, and she doesn't want to enter billing details in Google Ads. What should she do? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Use Trends and a free keyword tool, and note "No data yet"
- b) Pay for an ad campaign, because this lesson can't work without one
- c) Ask an AI tool for the volumes, and copy them into her sheet
- d) Stop keyword research until the site has a year of its data

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Other free sources can still show patterns and estimates. She doesn't need to run ads, and she should note what's missing.
**Hint:** Which sources need neither your site's data nor billing details?

### Q6. A client's slide says a phrase gets 90,000 searches a month, with no source. What should you do first? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Use it in your plan, as the client knows their own market well
- b) Halve it to be on the safe side, and then use that number instead
- c) Ask an AI tool to confirm the figure, and quote the tool's reply
- d) Treat it as unsupported, and look the phrase up in a real source

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** With no tool, place or date, it's unsupported. Look it up yourself. If your figures aren't about the same size, reject it and show what you found.
**Hint:** Follow the flowchart from the top.

### Q7. Spot the error: "AI gave me 40,500 searches a month. A number that exact must come from real data." *(O3 · spot the error)*
- a) Search volumes are only ever measured over a full year
- b) An exact-looking number isn't evidence without a source
- c) AI tools can only give volumes for informational phrases
- d) The number is fine, as long as it's rounded down to 40,000

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** AI can produce precise-looking figures with nothing behind them. Trace a number to a tool, a place and a date, or reject it.
**Hint:** What would make the number trustworthy?

### Q8. Spot the error: "This phrase isn't in autocomplete, so nobody searches it. Delete it from the list." *(spot the error · review: m03-l02)*
- a) Autocomplete shows every search, so check it a second time
- b) Phrases can only be deleted once they've been used on a page
- c) Rare searches may not show up, so mark it Unconfirmed instead
- d) It should be deleted, but only after asking an AI tool about it

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** As you saw in Lesson 3.2, Unconfirmed doesn't mean wrong. Plenty of real searches are too rare to appear in autocomplete.
**Hint:** What does a missing prediction really prove?
