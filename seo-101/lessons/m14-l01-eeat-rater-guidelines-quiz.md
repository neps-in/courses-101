# M14.L01 Quiz — E-E-A-T Through a Rater's Eyes

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-10 against the Search Quality Rater Guidelines PDF (September 11, 2025) and Google Search Central's helpful content, generative AI content and third-party SEO pages.

### Q1. What happens when a quality rater gives your page a low rating? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Your page drops in rankings within a few days
- b) Google sends a warning to your Search Console account
- c) Nothing directly: ratings test Google's systems
- d) The rating is added to your site's E-E-A-T score

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** "No single rating can directly impact how a particular webpage, website, or result appears in Google Search" (§0.1). Ratings measure whether Google's ranking systems work, like feedback cards from diners.
**Hint:** Think of the restaurant analogy: who cooks, and who fills in cards?

### Q2. Which part of E-E-A-T do the guidelines call the most important? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Trust
- b) Experience
- c) Expertise
- d) Authoritativeness

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** "The most important member at the center of the E-E-A-T family is Trust." Untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T however expert they seem.
**Hint:** The financial scam example: an expert scammer is still…?

### Q3. Which three sources of evidence do raters use to judge E-E-A-T? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Backlinks, domain age and the word count of each page
- b) Search volume, organic traffic and the bounce rate
- c) Author photos, trust badges and star ratings in the footer
- d) The site's own claims, others' views, the page

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** §3.4 lists "What the website or content creators say about themselves", "What others say about the website or content creators", and "What is visible on the page". The other options are SEO metrics or decorations, not the guidelines' evidence.
**Hint:** Self, others, page.

### Q4. Tomás runs `"Tomas Hardware" reviews -site:tomashardware.pt` and finds no independent results. What should he record? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Negative reputation: the page should be rated Low
- b) No reputation information, which is neutral for a small site
- c) A reason to write reviews of the shop on other websites
- d) Proof the shop needs to buy press mentions

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** §3.3.5: small websites "may have little or no reputation information. This is not indicative of high or low quality." Writing fake reviews or buying mentions would damage trust.
**Hint:** Is silence good news, bad news, or no news?

### Q5. Kavya wants to publish "Which yoga poses cure a slipped disc". How should a rater see this? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Clear YMYL health advice that must come from an expert
- b) Not YMYL, because yoga is a hobby topic
- c) Fine on Kavya's experience alone, since she teaches yoga
- d) In between, so any author and no sources are acceptable

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Treatment advice for a medical condition is clear YMYL health. §3.4.1 says "some types of YMYL information and advice must come from experts". Kavya's own recovery story could rest on experience; a "cure" claim needs a named doctor or physiotherapist.
**Hint:** Is it a personal story or medical advice?

### Q6. Which of Nila Coffee's pages is "definitely not YMYL"? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) "Does coffee raise blood pressure?"
- b) "Is caffeine safe during pregnancy?"
- c) "Coffee and diabetes: what to know"
- d) "How to brew pour-over at home"

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** A wrong brewing ratio harms no one. The other three are health questions where a wrong answer could affect someone's health decisions. "Many or most topics are not YMYL", but these three are.
**Hint:** What's the worst that happens if each page is wrong?

### Q7. Spot the error. An agency's report says: "Your E-E-A-T score is 54/100 according to our tool. Google's raters will review your site after we add trust badges." What's wrong? *(O1 · spot the error)*
- a) Nothing: raters review any site that improves its score
- b) The score is fine, but raters only review YMYL websites
- c) E-E-A-T isn't a score, and raters don't rate sites for ranking
- d) The score should be out of 10, as in the rater guidelines

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** E-E-A-T isn't a score, and "Third-party tools don't have access to our internal ranking data." Raters rate samples to test Google's systems; a rating can't move your page.
**Hint:** Two false claims in one sentence.

### Q8. Spot the error. Kavya's 40-page yoga site gets this advice: "You need a paid all-in-one SEO suite to check your own backlinks and clicks." What's wrong? *(review: m13-l06 · spot the error)*
- a) Nothing: own-site data is only available in paid suites
- b) Free tools cover her own clicks and backlinks
- c) Only Bing has backlink data for small websites
- d) Paid suites are banned under Google's spam policies

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and Ahrefs Free cover your own site's clicks and backlinks. Paid suites mainly add competitor data, scale and automation.
**Hint:** Whose data is free?
