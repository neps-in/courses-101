# M07.L04 Quiz — AI as a Helper: Where It Helps, Where It Fails, and the Fact-Check Pass

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 2 spot-the-error (Q8 is the spaced review) · pass mark 70% (6 of 8). Q1–Q7 are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3) so a failed answer points at the exact concept.
> Facts checked 2026-11-09 against Google's 2023 AI content post, its generative AI content guidance (updated 2026-10-01), the helpful-content page, the spam policies, the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (September 2025) and the Claude and Gemini help pages.

### Q1. What is Google's position on content made with AI? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) All AI content is spam and is removed from the index automatically
- b) AI content gets a small ranking boost because it is produced faster
- c) AI content is allowed only if the AI tool is listed as the author
- d) Appropriate use is fine; quality matters, not how it was produced

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "Appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our guidelines." And: "Using AI doesn't give content any special gains. It's just content." Google's focus is "on the quality of content, rather than how content is produced".
**Hint:** Google judges the result, not the tool.

### Q2. Why do AI tools sometimes produce statistics and quotes that don't exist? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) They copy them from fake websites that Google has already removed
- b) They predict a likely sequence of words rather than retrieving facts
- c) They are told by their makers to add numbers to every answer given
- d) They only invent facts when the user asks them for a long answer

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Google: "generative models don't retrieve facts, but predict a likely sequence of words based on their training data. Because of this, generative AI outputs may contain inaccuracies (also known as hallucinations)."
**Hint:** What does a language model actually do?

### Q3. Besides the body text, which parts of an AI-assisted page does Google say need the same review? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) Titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text
- b) Only the first paragraph, because that's what appears in snippets
- c) Nothing else; metadata isn't shown to people, so it doesn't matter
- d) Only the URL slug, because Google reads it before anything else

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** The review "also applies to metadata like <title> elements, meta description elements, structured data, and alternate texts for images, which can appear in Search results."
**Hint:** What can appear in search results?

### Q4. An AI draft quotes "Dr. Lena Ortiz, a leading nutrition researcher". Amara searches the exact quote in quotation marks and finds nothing. What should she do? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Keep the quote, but remove the doctor's name so nobody can complain
- b) Keep it, since the AI tool showed a link that looked like a source
- c) Cut the quote; with no original source, treat it as invented
- d) Rephrase the quote slightly so that it no longer counts as a quote

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** The exact-phrase search is the rater guidelines' own method. If the words appear nowhere, the quote is almost certainly made up. Anthropic warns that AI "can display quotes that may look authoritative or sound convincing, but are not grounded in fact."
**Hint:** No original, no quote.

### Q5. Nila's cold brew post was written from her own tests, but an AI assistant wrote most of the text from her notes. What does Google suggest about disclosure? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) Consider a short note on how AI was used, as readers may reasonably ask
- b) Name the AI tool as the author in the byline, so readers are informed
- c) Hide any AI use, because disclosure lowers the page's rankings
- d) Disclosure is a legal must for every page that any AI tool touched

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** AI disclosures "are useful for content where someone might think "How was this created?"" "Consider adding these when it would be reasonably expected." Google says an AI byline "is probably not the best way" to do it.
**Hint:** "Reasonably expected".

### Q6. Amara is asked to produce 80 AI pages called "Plumber in [suburb]", each with the same text and the suburb swapped. What does this risk? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) Nothing, as long as each page has a different title tag and URL
- b) A small duplicate-content warning that can be fixed with canonicals
- c) Nothing, because scaled content rules only apply to news websites
- d) Scaled content abuse: many pages made to rank, not to help users

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** Scaled content abuse "is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users", and examples include "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users".
**Hint:** Count the pages, then ask what each adds.

### Q7. Spot the error. Kavya writes: "I'll add 'Dr. Priya Rao, physiotherapist' as co-author with an AI headshot. Google can't tell, and it makes the page look more trustworthy." What's wrong? *(O3 · spot-the-error)*
- a) Nothing; a co-author is fine as long as the headshot looks realistic
- b) It's a fabricated creator profile, which Google calls deception
- c) The problem is only the headshot; a fake name alone would be fine
- d) She should list the AI tool as co-author instead of a made-up doctor

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** "Fabricating creator profiles (such as by using AI-generated headshots, made-up names, or false credentials …) is a form of deception." Use a real reviewer with real credentials, or none.
**Hint:** Is Dr. Rao a real person?

### Q8. Spot the error. *(review: m01-l06)* A forum post says: "Google bans AI content, so any page written with ChatGPT will be penalised." What's wrong with this claim?
- a) Nothing; Google has banned AI content since the 2024 core update
- b) Google bans only AI images, not AI-written text on web pages
- c) Google targets spam, not AI: pages made to manipulate rankings
- d) Google bans AI content only on pages that use structured data

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** AI content isn't banned; AI spam is (Lesson 1.6). "Using automation—including AI—to generate content with the primary purpose of manipulating ranking in search results is a violation of our spam policies." Helpful content made with AI is fine.
**Hint:** It's about purpose, not tools.
