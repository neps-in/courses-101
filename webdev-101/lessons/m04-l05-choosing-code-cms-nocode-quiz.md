# M04.L05 Quiz — Choosing How to Build: Code, CMS or No-Code

> 6 questions · 2 recall, 3 scenario, 1 spot-the-error · pass mark 70% (5 of 6). Questions are tagged with the objective they test (O1–O3).
> Prices change often; questions test the method, not today's prices (checked 2026-11-02).

### Q1. Which two checklist questions count double, because they decide daily life with the site? *(O1 · recall)*
- a) Budget and features, since money limits every other option
- b) Who edits and how often, since they shape every working week
- c) Ownership and exit, since leaving is the costliest step later
- d) Skills and features, since they decide what can be built at all

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Who will update the site, and how often, decide whether a code, CMS or builder workflow is livable. Budget, features, ownership and exit still matter, but a site that its editors can't or won't update fails first.
**Hint:** Think about a normal Tuesday with the site.

### Q2. When comparing options over three years, which cost is most often left out? *(O2 · recall)*
- a) The monthly or yearly platform price shown on the pricing page
- b) The domain name, which every option needs in some form or other
- c) The hours spent on updates and changes, times an hour's value
- d) The cost of the computer you use to write and edit the website

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Pricing pages list the subscription; nobody lists your time or the developer you'll call. Hours × hourly value often changes the ranking. The domain is usually counted, and the computer is the same for every option.
**Hint:** What isn't on any pricing page?

### Q3. A yoga teacher posts two classes a week, wants her assistant to publish posts too, and wants to own her content in case she moves host. Which fits best? *(O1 · scenario)*
- a) WordPress, for regular posts, two editors and an export
- b) A hand-coded static site, edited and pushed with VS Code
- c) A shop platform, so that classes can be sold as products
- d) A one-page builder, since she has only one page to update

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Frequent posting, two non-technical editors with roles, and a wish to own and move the site all point to a CMS such as WordPress (WordPress 101). Hand-coding needs code skills for every post; a shop platform is for stock and carts; she has more than one page.
**Hint:** Count the editors and the posts per week.

### Q4. A café owner updates a one-page menu twice a year; her nephew knows HTML. A salesperson suggests a $39-a-month shop builder. What's your advice? *(O2 · scenario)*
- a) Take the builder, because a monthly plan always includes support
- b) Hand-code it on free hosting; over three years it costs far less
- c) Use WordPress with a shop plugin, ready for online orders later
- d) Skip a website and rely only on a map listing and social media

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Rare edits, static content and someone with skills point to hand-coded static hosting: about a domain's cost over three years versus ~$1,400 for the builder. WordPress with a shop plugin over-builds; a map listing helps but doesn't replace a site she controls.
**Hint:** Multiply $39 by 36 months.

### Q5. A client chose a builder two years ago and now wants to move. What should the original recommendation have included? *(O3 · scenario)*
- a) A promise that builder prices would stay the same for years
- b) A note that every builder exports a site as editable code
- c) An exit line: what they would keep and what they'd rebuild
- d) A rule that the agency, not the client, holds the account

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** A good recommendation names the exit cost up front: content may export, but the design usually must be rebuilt. Prices change, many builders don't export editable code, and the client should always own the account.
**Hint:** Which of the seven questions is about leaving?

### Q6. Spot the error in this three-year cost line for a builder: "Year 1 $16 × 12 + free domain; Years 2–3 same as Year 1. Total: $576." *(O2 · spot-the-error)*
- a) The monthly price should be multiplied by 52 weeks per year
- b) Years 2–3 leave out the domain renewal and renewal prices
- c) Builders never charge monthly, so the whole line is invalid
- d) The total should be divided by three to give a fair figure

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** "Free domain" usually means year 1 only, and introductory prices often rise on renewal, so years 2–3 must use renewal prices and add the domain. Builders do bill monthly or yearly, and a three-year total is the figure to compare.
**Hint:** What ends after year 1?
