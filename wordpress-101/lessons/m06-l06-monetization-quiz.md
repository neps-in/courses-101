# M06.L06 Quiz — Monetization models and selling one digital product

> 7 questions · 3 recall, 2 scenario, 1 spot-the-error, 1 spaced-review question from M06.L03.
> Thresholds and fees checked on providers' pages on 2026-11-01; they change often. Not tax advice.

### Q1. A blog has 2,000 visits a month and 400 engaged newsletter subscribers. Which models usually fit best to start?
- a) Premium display ads through a top ad network
- b) Affiliate links, one digital product, and small sponsorships
- c) A full WooCommerce store with many products
- d) A paid membership with weekly exclusive videos

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Small audiences earn through trust, not volume. Premium networks have traffic minimums (Raptive: 25,000 pageviews/month), and free ad networks pay very little at this size. A full store or a weekly membership is a lot to maintain before you know what readers will buy.
**Hint:** Which models depend on trust rather than traffic?

### Q2. What does a **merchant of record** do for you when you sell a digital product?
- a) It writes the product for you
- b) It's the legal seller to the buyer, so it handles sales tax or VAT
- c) It guarantees a minimum number of sales
- d) It hosts your WordPress site

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** A merchant of record (e.g. Gumroad, per its pricing page) sells to the buyer on your behalf and deals with collecting and paying sales tax and VAT. That's why it's the easiest start for selling digital products internationally, though it takes a bigger cut per sale.
**Hint:** Who is the buyer legally paying?

### Q3. Which item belongs on a Work With Me page?
- a) Audience numbers with a date, such as "as of November 2026"
- b) Your highest-ever monthly visits, without a date
- c) Logos of brands you'd like to work with one day
- d) A promise of guaranteed sales for sponsors

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Brands want real, current numbers, so date them. Only show past partners you've really worked with (with permission), and never promise results you can't control.
**Hint:** Honest and current beats impressive.

### Q4. Sara wants to sell one $9 PDF planner to readers worldwide. What's the simplest route?
- a) Install WooCommerce, a tax plugin and a payment gateway
- b) Email the PDF by hand after people send money by bank transfer
- c) Create it on a merchant-of-record checkout and link a Button block to it
- d) Put the PDF in the Media Library and post the file's URL

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** For one product, a hosted checkout handles payment, delivery and (with a merchant of record) tax. WooCommerce is a full shop to maintain, manual delivery doesn't scale, and a public file URL means anyone can download it for free.
**Hint:** One product doesn't need a whole shop.

### Q5. Tom applied to Amazon Associates, added a few links, and five months later has made one sale. What's likely to happen?
- a) Nothing; there's no deadline
- b) It may be closed without 3 qualifying sales in 180 days
- c) Amazon will pay a fixed monthly fee instead
- d) His account becomes permanent after one sale

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Amazon's help pages say new Associates have 180 days to make 3 qualifying sales before the application is reviewed; without them it can be closed. He can reapply later once his site gets more traffic. Put affiliate links on posts that already get visits.
**Hint:** Amazon reviews new accounts after a fixed window.

### Q6. Spot the error: Max's monetization plan says:
> 1. Start with affiliate links for the 3 tools I actually use.
> 2. Put the affiliate disclosure in a line at the very bottom of each post.
> 3. Publish Work With Me with dated numbers and "from" prices.
> 4. Sell one checklist through a hosted checkout linked from my most-visited post.

Which step is wrong?
- a) Step 1
- b) Step 2
- c) Step 3
- d) Step 4

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Disclosures must be clear and appear before readers act, near the link, not hidden at the bottom of the page (details in M08.L04). The other steps match the lesson.
**Hint:** When should a reader find out you earn from a link?

### Q7. (review: m06-l03) You add a Gumroad checkout to your site. What should you update?
- a) Nothing; Gumroad has its own privacy policy
- b) Your privacy policy, naming the checkout provider your buyers' data goes to
- c) Your Discussion settings
- d) Your RSS feed settings

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Every new service that receives readers' or buyers' personal data belongs under *Who we share your data with* in your privacy policy (M06.L03). Add any plugin you installed to your plugin log too (M06.L01).
**Hint:** Who else now handles your readers' data?
