# M05.L03 Quiz — Writing for search intent: headings, internal links and answer-first paragraphs

> 7 questions · 3 recall, 2 scenario, 1 spot-the-error, 1 spaced-review question from M04.L05.
> Checked against Google Search Central, the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (2025-09-11) and WordPress 7.0 on 2026-10-31.

### Q1. What's the most reliable way to find out the search intent behind a query?
- a) Ask an AI chatbot what people usually mean by the query
- b) Search it in a private window and see what the top results have in common
- c) Pick the intent that matches the post you've already written
- d) Check how many times the query appears in your post's text

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** The top results are the best clue to what Google is rewarding for that query today: the intent, format and angle that work. AI tools can't reliably see current results in your country, and your existing post or keyword count says nothing about what searchers want.
**Hint:** Where can you see what Google already rewards for that query?

### Q2. What does Google say about word count?
- a) Posts under 1,000 words are treated as thin content
- b) Posts should be at least 1,500 words to rank for competitive queries
- c) It has no preferred word count; writing to one is a warning sign
- d) Longer posts are always preferred for AI Overviews

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** In "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content", Google asks whether you're writing to a word count because you've heard it has a preferred one, and answers "No, we don't." Cover what the searcher needs, then stop.
**Hint:** It's one of Google's "search engine-first" warning signs.

### Q3. Which link text follows Google's anchor text guidance best?
- a) To learn more, **click here**.
- b) Read my guide to **making a soft-boiled ramen egg**.
- c) I wrote about this **in another post**.
- d) **Easy ramen egg recipe soft boiled ramen egg how to**

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Good anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise and relevant to the page it links to. Google lists "click here" and similar as too generic; "in another post" doesn't say where it goes; option d is keyword stuffing.
**Hint:** Could a reader guess the target page from the linked words alone?

### Q4. Tomás's post *Best budget microphones* opens with two paragraphs about how he started podcasting. The top results for "best budget microphone for podcasting" are comparison lists with prices. What's the best first change?
- a) Add the exact query to every H2 so Google sees the match clearly
- b) Open with his top picks, their prices and who each suits; story after
- c) Make the post longer than every competing result, adding more history
- d) Delete the post, because blogs can't rank against shopping comparison sites

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Searchers want to compare and choose, and the winning format is a comparison list. An answer-first opening gives searchers (and AI answers) the verdict straight away; the story can follow. Keyword-stuffed headings and extra length don't fix a mismatch, and blogs can rank with good comparisons.
**Hint:** What does the searcher want in the first few seconds?

### Q5. Ama publishes a new guide, *How to repot a snake plant*. Which action best helps readers and Google discover it through internal links?
- a) Add a link to it from her older, related post *Snake plant care for beginners*
- b) Add ten links from the new guide to her homepage
- c) Set every link in the guide to open in a new tab
- d) Nothing: the SEO plugin's sitemap already lists it, so links aren't needed

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** Archive pages link to a new post only until newer posts push it back. A link **to** it from a related older post, with descriptive anchor text, gives readers and crawlers a lasting path and signals it matters. Google says every page you care about should have a link from at least one other page. A sitemap helps discovery but doesn't replace links; links *from* the guide help other pages; new tabs don't help.
**Hint:** Links *in* vs links *out*.

### Q6. Spot the error: Leo's checklist for rewriting a post says:
> 1. Search the target query privately and note the intent, format and angle of the top results.
> 2. Put the direct answer in the first 2–3 sentences.
> 3. Use an H4 for small sub-points under H2s, because H3 looks too big in my theme.
> 4. Add internal links with anchor text that describes each target post.

Which step is wrong?
- a) Step 1
- b) Step 2
- c) Step 3
- d) Step 4

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Headings show structure, not size. Jumping from H2 to H4 skips a level, which confuses screen-reader users and makes the outline harder to follow. Use H3 under H2 and change its size in **Styles** (M04.L02) if needed.
**Hint:** What does the Outline panel warn about?

### Q7. (review: m04-l05) Before publishing, how can Leo check that his post's headings are in order without installing anything?
- a) **Settings → Reading → Heading order**
- b) The editor's **Document Overview → Outline**
- c) **Appearance → Editor → Styles → Typography**
- d) Preview the post on mobile and count the font sizes

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Document Overview → Outline lists every heading in order and marks skipped levels with "(Incorrect heading level)". Typography styles change the look, not the structure; there's no Reading setting for headings.
**Hint:** It's in the block editor's top toolbar.
