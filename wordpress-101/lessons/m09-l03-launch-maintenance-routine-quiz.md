# M09.L03 Quiz — Launch day and your 30-day maintenance routine

> 8 questions · 3 recall, 3 scenario, 1 spot-the-error, 1 spaced-review question from M09.L02.
> Checked against Search Console Help, Google Search Central, Bing Webmaster Tools help and GA4 Help on 2026-11-01.

### Q1. On launch day, how should you get your pages in front of Google?
- a) Submit the sitemap; request indexing for two key pages
- b) Request indexing in URL Inspection for every page and post on the site
- c) Wait for Google to find the site on its own; there's nothing to submit
- d) Email Google support with a list of your new URLs and ask them to add them

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** The sitemap tells Google about every URL at once. Google says there's *"a quota for submitting individual URLs"*, so **Request indexing** is for a few key or updated pages, not the whole site.
**Hint:** One file for everything, a button for a few.

### Q2. Which list is the **weekly** routine in the lesson?
- a) Timed test restore, user and plugin audit, cookie re-scan
- b) Renewals, legal page review, rebuild the content plan
- c) Backup ran, updates, comments and spam, publish per plan
- d) Data review with three actions, Site Health and an offline backup

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** Weekly care is short: backups ran, updates done safely, comments moderated, the planned post published. The restore and audit list is quarterly, renewals and legal pages are yearly, and the data review is monthly.
**Hint:** What needs checking every few days, not every few months?

### Q3. What do campaign tags like `?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=launch` do?
- a) They stop search engines from indexing the tagged page
- b) They make the link load faster on mobile devices
- c) They hide the link from people who aren't subscribers
- d) They show analytics which announcement sent the visit

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** UTM parameters label where a visit came from (source, medium, campaign). GA4 reads them automatically, so you can compare the launch email with each social post.
**Hint:** Source, medium, campaign.

### Q4. Priya wants to make her launch bigger by importing her 600 LinkedIn connections into her newsletter and sending them the launch email. What's the problem?
- a) Newsletter tools can't send to more than 500 people at once
- b) They never signed up, so it breaks consent and provider terms
- c) LinkedIn contacts can only be emailed through LinkedIn's own paid tools
- d) There's no problem as long as she adds an unsubscribe link

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** Only people who subscribed through her form consented to her emails (M06.L03). She can share the launch on LinkedIn itself, as a post on her own account.
**Hint:** Who asked to hear from her?

### Q5. Sam launched this morning. The sitemap shows **Couldn't fetch**, and his site shows a "Coming soon" page in a private window. What should he do first?
- a) Delete the sitemap and submit each post URL one by one instead
- b) Switch SEO plugins, because his current one must be broken
- c) Turn off coming-soon mode, check the sitemap, resubmit
- d) Wait a month for Google to retry before changing anything

**Answer:** c
**Explanation:** A coming-soon page blocks visitors and crawlers alike, so Google can't read the sitemap. Turning it off and checking `/sitemap_index.xml` in a private window usually fixes it.
**Hint:** What does a logged-out visitor see?

### Q6. Ana's Monday "weekly care" event keeps getting skipped because it's at 8 am, when she's on the school run. What's the best fix?
- a) Move it to a free slot and trim it to the essentials
- b) Delete the weekly event and do everything at the yearly check
- c) Turn on auto-updates for everything so she never needs to check again
- d) Keep it at 8 am and add three more reminders before it

**Answer:** a
**Explanation:** A routine only works if it fits your week. Move it, and if time is tight keep the backup check and updates weekly and push the rest to the monthly review.
**Hint:** The calendar should fit her life, not the other way round.

### Q7. Spot the error in Leo's launch-day plan:
> 1. Untick **Discourage search engines** and turn off coming-soon mode.
> 2. Submit `sitemap_index.xml` in Search Console and import the site into Bing.
> 3. Request indexing for the homepage and his first cornerstone.
> 4. Skip the calendar events: he'll remember to check the site when he has time.

- a) Step 1: Discourage search engines should stay ticked until day 30
- b) Step 2: Bing can't import sites that are verified in Search Console
- c) Step 3: the homepage should never be submitted for indexing
- d) Step 4: without reminders, the routine soon stops

**Answer:** d
**Explanation:** "If it isn't in the calendar, it won't happen." Backups failing or updates piling up are rarely visible until something breaks. Steps 1–3 follow the lesson.
**Hint:** What makes a routine happen?

### Q8. (review: m09-l02) Which post should Leo's launch email point readers to first?
- a) His newest post, whatever its topic happens to be
- b) His first cornerstone post
- c) His privacy policy, so readers know how their data is used
- d) His category archive page for Uncategorized

**Answer:** b
**Explanation:** The cornerstone is his most complete, most useful post, and supporting posts link to it. Sending launch traffic there makes the best first impression and leads readers into the cluster.
**Hint:** Which post is the guidebook at the front of the shelf?
